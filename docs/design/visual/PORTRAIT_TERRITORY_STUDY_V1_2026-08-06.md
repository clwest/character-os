# Portrait Territory Study

## v1 — the benchmark before commitment

*Anchored in [`PORTRAIT_LANGUAGE_V1_2026-08-06.md`](PORTRAIT_LANGUAGE_V1_2026-08-06.md). Portrait Language locks the principles every portrait must satisfy. This study defines five candidate rendering directions and the empirical benchmark that must be conducted before any of them is selected. The study does not pick a winner from prose alone; it defines the testing sequence that will pick one from evidence.*

---

## Guiding principle

> **The purpose of the portrait system is not to create the most beautiful teammate. It is to create the teammate customers trust, recognize, and enjoy working beside over years of repeated use.**

Every candidate direction, every benchmark dimension, and every decision downstream of this study should be evaluated against that sentence. When a beautiful direction fails long-term trust or recognition, it fails the purpose. When a plain direction sustains both, it succeeds.

---

## 1. Purpose

Portrait Language locks the principles every portrait must satisfy. It deliberately does not lock the aesthetic register — the exact realism level, the painterly-vs-photographic choice, the animation intensity — because those decisions require testing against real provider outputs.

This study defines five candidate rendering directions and the benchmark that will select among them. Every characterization of a candidate below is a **hypothesis to validate**, not a claim to defend. The winner emerges from provider qualification and full-product feasibility review, not from persuasive prose.

The study is hypothesis-driven throughout.

---

## 2. Governing constraints (from Portrait Language)

Every candidate direction must satisfy:

- Identity continuity across every surface a teammate appears on.
- Framing families discipline.
- Same-room lighting coherence.
- Static (10s), Motion (30s), and Speech (60s) approval tests.
- Cross-context side-by-side identity review.
- Mouth, teeth, and hair identity-critical stability.
- Presence-without-performance idle behavior.
- Coherence of realism across skin, eyes, teeth, hair, and clothing.
- Diversity as *distinct people,* not interchangeable variants.
- Provider-qualification benchmark before use.

Any candidate that cannot demonstrably satisfy the constraints in empirical testing is removed regardless of how well it reads on paper.

# Five candidate rendering directions

Every characterization below is a **hypothesis for the benchmark to confirm or refute.** Language throughout uses *"expected to,"* *"likely to,"* *"hypothesized to,"* *"must be confirmed."*

---

## Direction A — Softened Photographic

### Core idea

Portraits derived from photorealistic rendering with intentional softening applied. The register of a considered portrait photograph — real-looking, believable, individual — treated with slight diffusion that removes the sharpness of documentary photography and the polish of corporate headshot photography.

**Clarification on "softened":** the softening refers to **edge treatment and tonal rendering** — not cosmetic smoothing. Real skin texture remains. Beauty-filter smoothness, jaw-slimming, and eye-widening are forbidden per Portrait Language and remain forbidden here.

### Realism position (hypothesis)

Expected to sit at the high-realism end of the four candidate band. Alex expected to read as a real-looking person, softened.

### Expected strengths (hypotheses to validate)

- **Identity stability across systems** is hypothesized to be high; must be confirmed during provider testing.
- **Live Conversation compatibility** is hypothesized to be strong; live-avatar systems today are natively photorealistic.
- **Coherence of realism across skin, eyes, teeth, hair, clothing** is hypothesized to be more attainable than in stylized registers because all elements can sit at the same fidelity level.
- **Customer video compatibility** is hypothesized to be strong when the customer's video content sits next to Alex.
- **Provider ecosystem** as of the date of this document currently offers many candidate technologies; this is a technology-availability observation, not a permanent property.

### Risks (must be confirmed during provider testing)

- **Anti-uncanny insulation** is hypothesized to be lowest. Any inconsistency in a photorealistic register is loudly visible.
- **Stock-photo drift** is a real risk if softening is over-applied.
- **Distinctness as *Character OS*** is hypothesized to be lowest; a softened-photographic Alex may resemble many other products.
- **Cultural specificity** — real photographs carry more cultural markers; localization pressure may be higher.

### Behavior across contexts (hypotheses)

- **Home:** unquestionably real-looking portrait, warm ambient.
- **Studio:** the same portrait animated for narration; motion fidelity must match still fidelity.
- **My Team:** cards of real-looking people; diversity requirement carries the *"generic AI faces"* risk if the generator is monotonous.
- **Live Conversation:** photorealistic live avatar; register aligns with current provider capabilities.

### Distinguishing signature (hypothesis)

**Naturalism.** Alex hypothesized to feel like a real considered portrait photograph, softened.

### Provider technology observation

Current provider ecosystem for both still generation and live-avatar rendering in this register is mature as of the date of this document. Vendor pool is relatively large; qualifying against Portrait Language's benchmark is the primary work.

### Illustrative Alex snapshot

Alex on Home reads as a real-looking person. Skin retains believable texture without pore-level sharpness. Hair reads as real. Eyes engaged, softly lit. Overall ambient softness signals *considered portrait* rather than *snapshot* — but the register remains recognizably photographic.

## Direction B — Cinematic Naturalistic

### Core idea

The register of naturally-lit contemporary portrait cinematography. Considered composition, careful lighting, subtle tonal grading appropriate to real-world environment. Real-looking, with the aesthetic care of a considered film frame from a small independent production.

**Clarifications on "cinematic":**

Explicitly rejected within this direction:

- Blockbuster color grading.
- Dramatic rim lighting.
- Shallow-depth glamour photography aesthetics.
- Teal-and-orange color science.
- Overproduced or over-stylized "movie-look" treatments.

The intended register is **naturally-lit contemporary portrait cinematography** — the way a good documentary or naturalistic-feature cinematographer would light a face in a real-world environment, not the way a summer blockbuster or fashion campaign would.

### Realism position (hypothesis)

Expected to sit at high realism, slightly less than Softened Photographic. Alex real, with visible aesthetic intention.

### Expected strengths (hypotheses to validate)

- **Emotional richness** is hypothesized to be high; the cinematic register carries warmth and depth natively.
- **Diverse portrait handling** is hypothesized to be strong; considered cinematography respects varied faces.
- **Celebration moments** may benefit especially; the register can carry weight when the customer stops to look.
- **Balance between "designed" and "real"** may be the most defensible; the product could feel considered without feeling artificial.
- **Lighting discipline** is native to the register.

### Risks (must be confirmed during provider testing)

- **Provider capability required** for consistent tonal treatment across every context is higher than Direction A.
- **Cinematic clichés** must be actively rejected; any drift toward blockbuster/rim-light/shallow-depth aesthetics disqualifies the output.
- **Speech animation** may drift toward cinema pacing rather than conversation pacing.
- **Real-time cinematic look** in Live Conversation may require custom pipelines beyond current provider defaults.

### Behavior across contexts (hypotheses)

- **Home:** cinematically-considered portrait; warm ambient light with subtle natural grading.
- **Studio:** the considered portrait animated at conversational pace.
- **My Team:** cards feel like a small ensemble of considered subjects.
- **Live Conversation:** technically demanding; must be confirmed with providers capable of consistent post-processing.

### Distinguishing signature (hypothesis)

**Considered natural light.** Alex hypothesized to feel *directed* rather than *captured* — warm, present, aesthetically intentional.

### Provider technology observation

Requires providers capable of consistent post-processing across every output. May require a grading layer atop a photorealistic generator. Live-avatar options in this register are currently narrower than Direction A.

### Illustrative Alex snapshot

Alex on Home has the register of a naturally-lit contemporary film frame — warm ambient light, real skin with cinematic softness, thoughtful natural color. Not stark. Not documentary. Not blockbuster. Composed.

## Direction C — Painterly Naturalistic

### Core idea

Portraits rendered with the sensibility of contemporary realist portrait painting. Recognizable as individual people — real skin tones and hair, real eyes, real features — but with a surface quality that reads as *considered portraiture* rather than *photography.*

**Clarification on "painterly":** the goal is **portrait interpretation** rather than obvious painted texture. The register should evoke *"someone considered this face"* — not *"visible brushwork applied over a face."* Oil-paint effects, canvas texture, and any explicitly-illustrated painterly treatments are outside the intended direction.

### Realism position (hypothesis)

Expected to sit at mid-to-high realism. Real proportions and identity, considered surface treatment.

### Expected strengths (hypotheses to validate)

- **Anti-uncanny insulation** is hypothesized to be high; considered surface treatment may forgive small artifacts that would fail in photorealism.
- **Distinctness as *Character OS*** is hypothesized to be strongest; the register is unlikely to resemble generic product output.
- **Static-render identity stability** is expected to be high; considered surfaces are inherently settled.
- **Stock-photo drift** is expected to be reduced to near-zero.

### Risks (must be confirmed during provider testing)

- **Provider ecosystem for animated and live-avatar portraiture in this register is currently thin** — this is a technology-availability observation, not an inherent aesthetic limitation. As the ecosystem matures, this risk may reduce.
- **May clash with customer video content** whose register is realistic; must be confirmed with real customer media in mixed layouts.
- **May prevent customer-provided realistic references from integrating** without additional transformation work.
- **Some customers may perceive the register as insufficiently "professional"** — must be tested with representative customer audiences.

### Behavior across contexts (hypotheses)

- **Home:** considered portrait; warm ambient; distinctive.
- **Studio:** the considered portrait animated for narration; motion fidelity is the primary technical unknown.
- **My Team:** cards of considered portraits; naturally distinct-looking.
- **Live Conversation:** the largest technical unknown of the five candidates; may currently require fallback to stable portrait with voice per Portrait Language §15.

### Distinguishing signature (hypothesis)

**Portrait interpretation.** Alex hypothesized to feel *considered* rather than *captured.*

### Provider technology observation

Current provider ecosystem for still-image portraits in this register is available. Provider ecosystem for animated real-time avatars in this register is currently limited. Bespoke rendering pipelines may be required today; this may change as providers evolve.

### Illustrative Alex snapshot

Alex on Home reads as a considered portrait. Face and hair rendered with clear artistic sensibility. Real skin tone, real expression, but a slightly-softened edge and subtly-generalized surface that suggests portrait interpretation rather than photography. Distinctive at a glance without reading as *painted-in-oil.*

## Direction D — Contemporary Portrait Illustration

### Core idea

The register of high-end contemporary editorial illustration — modern digital portraiture as practiced by editorial illustrators for major publications, or the register of Apple's occasional considered human imagery. Real human proportions and individual identity, rendered with a considered illustrative sensibility that reads as **interpretation** rather than reproduction. Sits between Painterly Naturalistic (which reads as painting) and Lightly Stylized Realism (which reads as illustration of a character).

**Clarification on "portrait illustration":** the intent is *editorial-quality portrait illustration of a real person* — recognizable individual, considered rendering, illustrative sensibility. Not marketing-page persona illustration. Not stock illustration. Not designed character.

### Realism position (hypothesis)

Expected to sit at mid realism. Real proportions and individual identity preserved; illustrative surface treatment.

### Expected strengths (hypotheses to validate)

- **Balance between distinctiveness and animation stability** is hypothesized to be stronger than Painterly Naturalistic while more distinctive than Lightly Stylized.
- **Preservation of "person" register** is hypothesized to be stronger than Lightly Stylized — the customer reads *"an illustration of a real person"* rather than *"a designed character."*
- **Identity consistency** may be strong; considered illustration can be tightly art-directed.
- **Cultural neutrality** may be stronger than Painterly (which can carry Western portrait-painting traditions) or Photographic (which carries real-world cultural markers). Must be tested.

### Risks (must be confirmed during provider testing)

- **Provider ecosystem for animated editorial-illustration portraits is currently unclear** — this is a technology-availability observation.
- **Drift toward corporate illustration aesthetics** — the register can slip into marketing-page personas if not tightly art-directed.
- **Live Conversation register** — real-time editorial illustration is largely untested at production quality; must be empirically evaluated.
- **May undermine Live Conversation's believability** more than Softened Photographic or Cinematic Naturalistic — the register asks the customer to trust an illustrated interlocutor with real-time speech.
- **Requires strong art direction** to distinguish from stock illustration.

### Behavior across contexts (hypotheses)

- **Home:** editorial-illustrated portrait, considered.
- **Studio:** illustrated portrait with subtle animation for narration.
- **My Team:** cards of considered illustrated portraits.
- **Live Conversation:** technical unknown; may require an adapted register or fallback.

### Distinguishing signature (hypothesis)

**Editorial interpretation.** Alex hypothesized to feel *drawn by a considered illustrator* — not painted, not photographed, but interpreted with editorial sensibility.

### Provider technology observation

Current provider ecosystem in still-image editorial illustration is available. Provider ecosystem for animated real-time portrait illustration is currently narrow. Custom pipelines are likely required today.

### Illustrative Alex snapshot

Alex on Home reads as a considered editorial portrait — the kind of illustration a magazine might commission for a profile piece. Recognizable individual features. Real skin tone. Real hair. Rendered with clear illustrative sensibility that reads as *interpretation* rather than *painting* or *photograph.* Distinctive without reading as obviously stylized.

## Direction E — Lightly Stylized Realism

### Core idea

Realistic proportions and features with mild abstraction applied. Real individual identity preserved; detail smoothed for animation stability.

**Clarification on the stylization ceiling:**

> **If a viewer immediately describes the teammate as a *character* rather than a *person,* the rendering has exceeded the acceptable stylization limit.**

The register must remain identifiable as *"a real-looking person, mildly abstracted"* — never as *"a designed character who is realistic-adjacent."*

### Realism position (hypothesis)

Expected to sit at mid realism. Real proportions, individual identity, visible stylization.

### Expected strengths (hypotheses to validate)

- **Animation compatibility** is hypothesized to be high; mild abstraction is native to 3D and 2D character-animation systems.
- **Motion consistency** across still and moving contexts is hypothesized to be strong.
- **Cost and performance** are expected to be favorable across surfaces.
- **Anti-uncanny valley resistance** is expected to be high; stylization is a well-established anti-uncanny strategy.

### Risks (must be confirmed during provider testing)

- **Mascot drift risk** is hypothesized to be highest; one wrong stylistic choice could push the register into character-illustration territory.
- **May feel less like a person** and more like a designed character — the primary failure mode for this direction.
- **Cultural presentation risk** — illustrations often encode cultural stereotypes more visibly than photographs; must be tested carefully.
- **Weakens "person you know" register** in favor of "character you recognize" — must be validated against the guiding principle.
- **Live Conversation believability** may be undermined; a stylized live avatar reads differently than a real face.
- **Customer-provided realistic references** may not integrate cleanly.

### Behavior across contexts (hypotheses)

- **Home:** stylized-realistic portrait; consistent, distinct.
- **Studio:** animates gracefully.
- **My Team:** cards of stylized portraits; cheapest to maintain.
- **Live Conversation:** technically native to animation systems, but the emotional register is uncertain.

### Distinguishing signature (hypothesis)

**Identity-preserved stylization.** Alex hypothesized to feel *drawn* — but as a portrait of a real person, not as a designed character.

### Provider technology observation

Stylized rendering pipelines are established in game-adjacent and animation-adjacent domains. Provider ecosystem leans toward gaming or animated-character use cases rather than product-teammate use cases. Strong art direction is essential to avoid character-design register.

### Illustrative Alex snapshot

Alex on Home reads as a considered illustration of a real person. Recognizable individual features. Believable skin tone. Real hair. Mild abstraction that reads as *illustrated* rather than *photographed.* Not cartoonish. Not exaggerated. Recognizable as a person.

# The benchmark

Every candidate direction that advances beyond initial screening must be tested through the following empirical program. The benchmark is the decision-making instrument; this document defers to it.

## 3. Extended evaluation dimensions

Portrait Language's static, motion, and speech tests are the minimum. This study adds two dimensions and a broader operational surface.

### 3.1 Cross-modal continuity

> *Does the teammate still feel like exactly the same person when transitioning between static portrait, idle motion, narration, and live conversation?*

Many systems perform well individually and fail during transitions. A candidate passes only when the identity survives the transitions between modes, not just within each mode.

### 3.2 Transition testing across surfaces

Character OS relies heavily on movement between surfaces. Test the full identity survival across:

```
Home → Studio → Live Conversation → My Team → Home
```

Reviewers evaluate whether identity holds through each transition. Any transition that reads as *"the teammate changed"* fails the candidate.

### 3.3 Operational dimensions (not just visual)

Visual fidelity is one criterion; the benchmark must evaluate the full operational picture:

- **Latency** — time from action to render.
- **Rendering consistency** — output stability across repeated generations of the same identity specification.
- **Startup time** — how long before a teammate becomes present on a surface.
- **Memory usage.**
- **GPU requirements.**
- **Mobile performance.**
- **Fallback behavior** under provider degradation.
- **Recovery** after network interruption or provider failure.

A visually excellent portrait that takes six seconds to appear fails Character OS.

### 3.4 Long-term familiarity testing

Character OS is not a one-session experience. Test the same teammate:

- **First impression** — reviewer sees the teammate for the first time.
- **After one day** — reviewer returns.
- **After one week** — sustained exposure.
- **Simulated repeated exposure** — accelerated familiarity testing.

Many attractive portrait styles become visually tiring over time. The purpose is to identify fatigue before it damages retention.

### 3.5 Observer fatigue testing

Reviewers watch:

- 10 minutes of idle portrait.
- Repeated conversation clips.

Then answer: *"Did anything become irritating?"*

Fatigue often reveals what first impression conceals.

### 3.6 Emotional neutrality testing

Portraits must remain believable across the full expression vocabulary:

- Neutral rest.
- Listening.
- Consideration.
- Warm response.

Many systems succeed only at smiling faces. A candidate must remain trustworthy at every expression, not only at the flattering ones.

### 3.7 Consistency under regeneration

Generate the same canonical teammate multiple times. Answer:

> *Can this provider recreate the same teammate reliably?*

Identity persistence across generations matters more than any single excellent render.

### 3.8 Demographic robustness

Do not benchmark only Alex. Generate multiple teammates varying naturally in:

- Age.
- Skin tone.
- Hair texture and length (including hair loss).
- Facial structure.
- Body type where visible.
- Gender presentation.
- Cultural presentation.
- Facial asymmetry.
- Teeth and smile characteristics.
- Visible disability where deliberately selected.

Some rendering directions fail only once diversity increases. The benchmark must expose these failures before selection, not after.

### 3.9 Provider independence

For each candidate direction, test at least three providers where technically possible. The benchmark is selecting a **rendering direction**, not a company.

```
Softened Photographic
  ├── Provider A
  ├── Provider B
  └── Provider C

Cinematic Naturalistic
  ├── Provider A
  ├── Provider B
  └── Provider C

...and so on
```

A direction that succeeds only on one vendor is a vendor commitment, not a direction commitment.

### 3.10 Failure behavior evaluation

Every candidate must be intentionally tested under degraded conditions:

- Dropped frames.
- Network slowdown.
- Reduced resolution.
- Lighting mismatch between generation and display environment.
- Expression interpolation failure.
- Partial render recovery.

Portrait Language §15 requires that degradation preserve trust. The benchmark verifies whether each direction survives degradation with dignity intact.

### 3.11 Trust as a first-class criterion

Reviewers evaluate:

- *Would I trust this teammate with my business?*
- *Would I enjoy working beside this teammate daily?*
- *Does this teammate feel dependable?*
- *Would I return tomorrow?*

These questions are **not identical to realism** or to absence of uncanny artifacts. A teammate can be technically flawless and fail trust. A teammate can carry small visible imperfections and pass trust. Trust is its own criterion.

### 3.12 Character OS distinctiveness

Distinctness is not the same as artistic uniqueness. The distinctness question is:

> *If this portrait appeared without logos or product context, would someone recognize the teammate experience?*

Recognizability of *the teammate experience* is what matters — not visual novelty. A rendering can be visually unique and unrecognizable as Character OS; a rendering can be visually restrained and unmistakably Character OS. The benchmark evaluates the second, not the first.

## 4. Weighted evaluation categories

Not every benchmark dimension counts equally. Without weighting, reviewers may unintentionally choose *"beautiful"* over *"usable."*

### 4.1 Critical (a candidate that fails any of these is removed)

- Identity continuity across every surface and every transition.
- Speech stability (mouth, teeth, lip contour, identity during speaking).
- Trust review (would customers trust and return).
- Provider reliability (candidates dependent on a single fragile vendor pipeline are non-viable).

### 4.2 Important (strong influence on selection)

- Warmth.
- Distinctness as *Character OS* (not brand novelty).
- Aesthetic character consistent with the visual language brief.
- Long-term familiarity (does not fatigue).
- Demographic robustness.
- Cross-modal continuity.

### 4.3 Nice-to-have (tie-breakers)

- Artistic uniqueness.
- Cinematic grading quality.
- Visual novelty.
- Peer-product comparability.

Reviewers must not confuse Nice-to-have wins for Important wins, and Important wins for Critical wins. A candidate that scores highest on Nice-to-have and fails a Critical is disqualified.

---

## 5. Benchmark exit criteria

A candidate direction **advances** in the benchmark only if it passes every required gate. It is **removed** if it fails any required gate, regardless of aesthetic appeal.

**Required gates (all mandatory):**

- Identity continuity (across surfaces and transitions).
- Static approval (10s test).
- Motion approval (30s test).
- Speech approval (60s test).
- Trust review (the four trust questions).
- Transition review (Home → Studio → Live Conversation → My Team → Home).
- Consistency under regeneration.
- Demographic robustness across the diversity requirements.
- Failure-behavior review (degrades with dignity, per Portrait Language §15).

Discussion continues only among candidates that clear every gate. A candidate that fails one gate is removed from consideration.

---

## 6. Sequenced decision path

The final aesthetic choice does not happen at the visual-benchmark stage. Character OS should choose the direction that survives the complete product, not merely the art review.

**Sequence:**

1. **Visual benchmark** — the exit criteria above; produces a shortlist of directions that meet the visual and identity requirements.
2. **Engineering feasibility** — can the shortlisted directions be built and maintained by Character OS's engineering system?
3. **Runtime cost** — do the shortlisted directions meet the cost envelope for the product's scale?
4. **Accessibility review** — do the shortlisted directions serve accessibility needs (reduced motion, low-vision compatibility, cultural safety)?
5. **Product integration** — do the shortlisted directions integrate cleanly with the seven canonical screen specifications?
6. **Final territory selection** — the direction that survives every prior stage.

A direction that wins the art review but fails engineering feasibility is not the winner. A direction that meets the visual benchmark but exceeds the cost envelope is not the winner. A direction that passes every prior stage but breaks a canonical interaction specification is not the winner.

---

## 7. Initial testing recommendation (hypothesis-driven)

**Do not select a direction from this study alone.** The recommendation below is a testing hypothesis, not a preference.

### Primary hypothesis

Test **Softened Photographic (A)** and **Cinematic Naturalistic (B)** in the initial benchmark round. These are the two directions **currently hypothesized** to best serve Live Conversation — a load-bearing surface that must work in the chosen register — and to align with current provider ecosystem maturity.

This hypothesis must be **actively challenged** by the benchmark, not assumed.

### Secondary hypothesis

Test **Painterly Naturalistic (C)** and **Contemporary Portrait Illustration (D)** in parallel if a provider or bespoke pipeline can demonstrate viable animated and live-avatar output at benchmark quality. These directions may prove technically feasible even if the current provider ecosystem is thinner; test empirically rather than dismiss on ecosystem grounds.

### Deprioritized

**Lightly Stylized Realism (E)** should not be a first choice unless the primary and secondary hypotheses fail during benchmark. The mascot-drift risk is highest and the *"person you know"* register — central to the guiding principle — is hypothesized to be weakest.

If (E) is tested despite deprioritization, the reviewer's stylization ceiling (§Direction E clarification) is the primary disqualifier.

---

## 8. What this study does not decide

Explicitly deferred to the provider-qualification benchmark and subsequent decisions:

- **Winner among the five directions** — requires empirical outputs from real providers.
- **Specific rendering technology and vendor choice.**
- **Default Alex casting** — the actual face, hair, features, appearance of the pre-provisioned first teammate.
- **Canonical outfit direction** for the default Alex.
- **Background family specifics** (family is defined in Portrait Language; specific backgrounds require testing).
- **Whether Alex is a single universal person or several defaults** — flagged in Portrait Language §25 as a consequential product decision requiring explicit operator resolution.
- **Micro-expression library** implementation details.
- **Voice-to-face synchronization** provider testing.
- **Whether Contemporary Portrait Illustration (D) integrates with Live Conversation** at production quality — an open empirical question this study raises but does not resolve.
