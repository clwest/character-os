# Library — Screen Design

## v1 — canonical screen design under PRODUCT_IA_V1

*Anchored in [`PRODUCT_IA_V1_2026-08-06.md`](../PRODUCT_IA_V1_2026-08-06.md) §7. The gallery of accomplishments. Where the customer sees what they've built and decides what to do with it next.*

*Sibling to [`HOME_V1_2026-08-06.md`](HOME_V1_2026-08-06.md), [`ONBOARDING_V1_2026-08-06.md`](ONBOARDING_V1_2026-08-06.md), [`STUDIO_V1_2026-08-06.md`](STUDIO_V1_2026-08-06.md). Piece Detail is a separate screen (later in the sequence); Library covers only the list view. No wireframes.*

---

## 1. Purpose recap

Library is where the customer sees the body of work they've built and finds their way back into a piece — to review it, reuse it, remix it, or share it. It answers *"What have I made — and what should I do with it now?"*

**Scrolling through Library should feel like *"look what I've built."*** This is Library's guiding sentence and the primary embodiment of Philosophy #6 (the customer's work is displayed as pride, not inventory). Every design decision in this document is answerable by asking: *does this feel like pride, or like storage?*

Library does not exist in navigation until the customer's first publish. The concept isn't relevant before that.

---

## 2. Screen anatomy

Library has a single top-level layout whose density and controls scale with the size of the collection.

- **Top area:** small screen heading. Filter affordance (collapsed by default — see §7). Search input (only after search is warranted — see §7.3).
- **Main area:** cards.
- **Bottom area:** the *"Still in progress"* section for drafts.
- **Navigation:** the persistent nav frames Library (per HOME_V1 §3).

No sidebar. No settings gear. No breadcrumbs. No hero image. No decoration competing with the pieces.

---

## 3. Progressive display modes

The three progressive display modes shape density and control affordances (per IA §7.2). Mode boundaries are not visible to the customer — the shift happens between sessions as the collection grows.

### 3.1 Mode A — Small library (1–9 published pieces)

The most visually generous mode. Every piece deserves space.

- **Layout:** single-column vertical stack. Cards are large and native.
- **Top area:** screen heading only. No filter chip visible (the collapsed filter affordance is present but doesn't appear until Mode B, when there's meaningfully more than one filter's worth of pieces to organize).
- **Bottom area:** drafts section if drafts exist.

### 3.2 Mode B — Growing library (10–30 pieces)

- **Layout:** grid — 2 columns tablet, 3 columns desktop, single column mobile.
- **Top area:** screen heading + collapsed filter chip (§7.1).
- **Bottom area:** drafts section if drafts exist.

### 3.3 Mode C — Established library (30+ pieces)

- **Layout:** grid same as Mode B.
- **Top area:** screen heading + collapsed filter chip + search input (§7.3, when warranted).
- **Bottom area:** drafts section if drafts exist.

## 4. Card design

Cards are the primary object of Library. They must render the piece in its native form so the customer sees what they built — not thumbnails of the abstract concept.

### 4.1 Structural rule

**Always visible on a card:**
1. **Native media area** — the piece itself, rendered contextually (§4.3).
2. **Title** — the contextual, customer-facing name (never a generic noun).
3. **One metadata line** — combining publish date and, if multiple teammates, teammate byline.

**Optional secondary chips** — one at most in typical cases; two only if a piece is both a series member and popular:
- *Popular* (post-reveal, when meaningful)
- *New* (recently published, < 24h; fades naturally)
- *Part of [series name]* (in filtered "All" view when the piece belongs to a series)

**Forbidden clutter:**
- Multiple stacked status lines.
- Analytics numbers on the card.
- Publish destination badges.
- Teammate details beyond the byline.
- File-size, format, duration, or resolution metadata.
- Any icon-heavy metadata row.

### 4.2 Card behavior

- The **entire card is tappable** and opens Piece Detail. No separate "View" or "Open" button.
- Cards have subtle hover treatment on desktop: mild elevation + border-color shift. Not a scale animation.
- **No autoplay. No hover-play.** Consistent with HOME_V1.
- Cards do **not** contain editing controls, timelines, chapter navigation, or any affordance that turns Library into a mini-editor. Library invites; Piece Detail immerses.

### 4.3 Contextual rendering — cards have personalities

Each media form gets a rendering appropriate to it. Library should look like different kinds of creative work hanging on a wall.

**Video pieces:**
- Media area is dominant: large poster frame with explicit play affordance.
- Hover on desktop reveals playback controls (play, expand, duration). Hover never begins playback.
- Tap plays inline muted; the card's expand affordance opens Piece Detail with the full player.
- Text — title and metadata line — sits *below* the media, treated with restraint so the visual leads.

**Text-based pieces:**
- Typography dominates. The first two paragraphs render in comfortable reading type, with a soft fade at the bottom and a small *Read the whole thing →* link.
- Media area (in the video/audio sense) is absent; the text itself is the visual.
- Title and metadata sit above the text (a byline for the piece).

**Audio pieces:**
- Waveform preview dominates the visual weight, spanning the card width.
- Play affordance is prominent, centered on the waveform.
- Duration surfaces in the metadata line.

**Series:**
- Collage-style: a lead image (the newest piece's poster) plus 2–3 smaller thumbnails arranged with a designerly touch — not a strict grid, more like a small mosaic that reads as *"a group of things."*
- Caption: *"3 pieces in this series."*
- Title is the series' name.

The three renderings should look and feel visually distinct — a video card next to a text card next to an audio card next to a series should read as a **mixed exhibition**, not a uniform data grid.

## 5. Series cards

Series render as one collection card per §4.3 (Series).

**Behavior:**
- Tap opens Piece Detail for the series (which shows all member pieces in a dedicated layout — spec'd in Piece Detail).
- Once the customer has any series, a *Series* filter option becomes available in the expanded filter row (§7.1).

## 6. Drafts — the *"Still in progress"* section

Drafts live at the bottom of Library in a quieter zone.

**Layout:**
- Section heading: *"Still in progress"* — smaller and quieter than the main screen heading.
- Cards use the same design as published cards but with subtle differentiation (muted border; a small quiet *Draft* chip).
- **Each draft card carries a direct action affordance:** **[Continue →]** — visible on the card, positioned at the lower-right corner.
  - Tapping [Continue →] opens Studio directly at the draft's state, bypassing Piece Detail.
  - Tapping the rest of the card opens Piece Detail as normal (for inspection before continuing).
- If a draft is being finished in the background (from Studio's [Come back when it's ready] flow), the card carries a subtle status indicator: *Still being finished* — consistent with HOME_V1 State 3.

**Draft-card sort:**
Drafts are sorted by *last worked on* descending, independent of Library's global sort. Active work stays at the top of the drafts section.

**If no drafts exist:** the section doesn't render. No placeholder.

## 7. Filters, sort, and search

Library's organizational controls are quiet by default. They appear when needed.

### 7.1 The filter affordance (Mode B onward)

**Default state:** a single chip visible in the top area, labelled with the current view (*All* by default, or the active filter name when one is active). A small caret indicates expandability.

**Interaction:**
- Tap the chip → the row expands into a horizontal list of available filter options.
- Tap an option → row collapses; the newly active option becomes the visible chip.
- Tap the visible chip again to expand and choose *All* to return to unfiltered.

**Available filter options** (order priority when the row is expanded):

1. **All**
2. **Popular** *(post-reveal; §8)*
3. **Customer-vocabulary categories** *(learned; §7.2)*
4. **Media type filters** *(Videos / Posts / Audio — only shown when customer vocabulary hasn't emerged strongly, or as a secondary group)*
5. **Series** *(if any series exist)*
6. **[Teammate name]** *(if multiple teammates exist)*

Only one filter active at a time (plus the implicit *All*).

### 7.2 Customer-vocabulary categories

Character OS learns the customer's own vocabulary from their piece titles over time. When a pattern emerges — three or more pieces sharing a category word — that category becomes a filter chip.

Examples:
- A bakery might see: *Announcements / Specials / Welcome messages* (extracted from titles like *"Your Thursday special"* / *"Your grand opening announcement"* / *"Your welcome message"*).
- A dealership might see: *Walkarounds / Financing / Trade-ins / Testimonials.*
- A consultant might see: *Case studies / How-tos / Recaps.*

**Rules:**
- Categories emerge; they are never asked for.
- The customer never explicitly categorizes; the system notices patterns and offers the filter silently.
- Categories may retire quietly if they stop being used (design-level: engineering tunes the aging rules).
- Generic media-type filters (*Videos / Posts / Audio*) become secondary once customer-vocabulary categories are strong; they never dominate a customer's own language.

This is a design-level requirement. Engineering will define the exact detection and aging model, informed by pattern-memory conventions from HOME_V1 §4.5.

### 7.3 Sort

Sort is quiet — a small text-and-caret affordance to the right of the filter chip.

**Options (Library never exposes more than these):**
- **Recent** *(default — newest first, always)*
- **Popular** *(post-reveal only; §8)*

There is no *Oldest first* option. Nobody browses old marketing first.

### 7.4 Search

Search does not appear on a fixed piece-count threshold. It appears when the customer's behavior signals actual need.

**Trigger conditions** (any one activates the search input):
- The customer's library has grown large enough that scrolling becomes visibly effortful (design-level: engineering may tune based on scroll analytics, but 30+ pieces is a reasonable prior).
- The customer scrolls a long distance multiple times within a single session (a signal they're searching by browsing).
- The customer explicitly asks (via the Ask surface on Home, or through Alex conversationally: *"How do I find that video from July?"*).

Once triggered, search appears as a full-width input in the top area (max ~640px, centered) with placeholder *"Search your work"* and remains available thereafter.

**Search is semantic, not literal.**

The customer searching for *"Christmas"* should find pieces titled *Holiday sale*, *Santa event*, *December promotion* — even if the literal word never appears. Search matches concepts and intent, not just strings.

Semantic matching applies across title, script/body, visual direction, and teammate byline. Engineering implements via embeddings or equivalent; the design guarantees the behavior.

**Search interaction:**
- Debounced typing triggers search after ~300ms pause.
- Enter also submits.
- Search runs alongside active filter and sort.
- Clearing input returns to the unfiltered view.
- No search operators, no advanced search, no search history surface in v1.

### 7.5 Empty filter / search result

Soft messaging centered in the grid area:
- Filter empty: *"Nothing here yet."* + (if a filter is active) *"Try clearing the filter."* as a text link.
- Search empty: *"No matches for '[search term]'."*

## 8. Library-side reveals

Library has four reveals. Each surfaces without ceremony — never announced.

### 8.1 Popular

**Trigger (usage):** 5+ published pieces with meaningful audience signal *(design-level: engineering defines threshold for "meaningful")*.

**Introduction pattern:**
Alex introduces this conversationally *before* the chip appears in Library. During a Home visit or a natural conversational moment:

> *"People really responded to your last few pieces — want me to point out which ones are working best?"*

Two inline replies: **[Yes, show me]** / [Not right now].

If the customer accepts:
- A **[Popular]** filter option appears in the expanded filter row.
- The **[Popular]** sort option appears in the sort picker.
- Pieces meeting the "popular" bar carry a small *Popular* chip on their card.

If the customer declines: the reveal does not surface in Library. Alex may offer again in the future.

**Copy note:** *Popular* is preferred over *"Best performing"* — the latter reads as marketing-software analytics; the former reads as human recognition. Internally the concept may be called *best-performing*; the customer-facing word is always *Popular*.

### 8.2 Series view

**Trigger (usage):** first series created.

Once the customer creates their first series: a **[Series]** filter option appears in the expanded filter row. Series cards render throughout the standard "All" view.

### 8.3 Repurpose / remix

**Trigger:** ask-based, or usage-based at 5+ published pieces.

This reveal manifests primarily in Piece Detail (**[Remix this]** primary action). Library itself doesn't change; the card behavior remains the same.

### 8.4 Tidy up (batch mode)

**Trigger (usage):** 20+ pieces in Library, or ask-based (*"help me clean up my library"*).

**Framing:** the affordance is called **[Tidy up]** — not *"Select"*. Alex may frame it during the reveal:

> *"You've built up quite a collection — want me to help you tidy things up?"*

Entering tidy-up mode:
- A **[Tidy up]** affordance appears in the top area next to the filter chip (post-reveal).
- Long-press on a card (mobile) or Shift-click (desktop) also enters tidy-up mode.
- In tidy-up mode: cards gain check overlays; tapping toggles selection.
- A bottom action bar appears with actions and a selected count.

**Batch actions:**
- **[Archive]** — moves pieces out of the main gallery but preserves them; recoverable.
- **[Move to a series]** — opens a compact picker showing existing series or an option to create one.
- **[Restore]** — visible when the current filter shows archived pieces.
- **[Done]** — exits tidy-up mode.

**No [Discard] action in tidy-up.** Delete exists in Piece Detail for individual pieces (with a 30-day soft-delete grace period). Batch operations remain respectful. Very little marketing content is truly garbage.

## 9. Milestone celebrations

Library celebrates milestones quietly. Not achievements. Not gamification. Recognition.

**When they appear:**
- The customer's **first published piece** — the celebration on Home covers this; Library echoes it subtly the first time it's opened (see State 1 in §11).
- The customer's **tenth piece** — a small quiet note near the screen heading: *"Your tenth piece."*
- **A year ago today** — when a piece from exactly one year prior exists, Library may surface a small quiet callout at the top: *"A year ago today: your grand opening video."* Tappable, opens that piece.
- **First series completed** — a small quiet note surfaces the first time Library is opened after the series' final piece is published.

**Rules:**
- Milestones are **rare**. No more than one visible at a time.
- Milestones are **quiet**. Never a modal, never a full-screen takeover, never confetti.
- Milestones **fade** after a session — they appear the first time Library is opened after the milestone is reached, and they don't persist across sessions unless the customer engages with them.
- Milestones **dismiss** without ceremony. A small × affordance suffices; scrolling past also dismisses.
- Milestones are **human**. Not counts, not medals, not badges. Just recognition.

This makes Library feel like a creative history rather than storage.

## 10. State preservation on return

**Returning from Piece Detail restores Library exactly as the customer left it.**

Preserved state includes:
- Active filter selection (or unfiltered "All").
- Sort selection.
- Search input contents (if search was active).
- Scroll position.
- Tidy-up mode active state and current selections (best-effort).
- Milestone dismissal state (if a milestone was showing and was dismissed, it stays dismissed).

The customer should feel like they stepped into a room and came back — not like the room reloaded.

**Persistence scope:**
- **Within-session:** always preserved (memory-cheap).
- **Across-session:** filter/sort/search do not persist by default; Library opens with unfiltered *All* and *Recent* sort at the top of the grid at each new session.
- **Batch selection:** cleared across sessions (a selection made yesterday should not persist to today).

## 11. All states enumerated

### State 1 — Library's first appearance (immediately after first publish)
- Reached via nav item after Home's celebration sequence has revealed Library.
- Mode A layout.
- Single card: the just-published piece. Large. Prominent play affordance.
- **First-piece milestone** callout appears quietly at the top: *"Your first piece."*
- No drafts section unless a draft existed before publishing.

### State 2 — Small library (Mode A, 1–9 pieces)
- Vertical stack of large native cards.
- No filter or search affordances.
- Drafts section at bottom if any.

### State 3 — Growing library (Mode B, 10–30 pieces)
- Grid layout.
- Collapsed filter chip visible in top area.
- Drafts section at bottom if any.

### State 4 — Established library (Mode C, 30+ pieces)
- Grid layout.
- Filter chip + sort control + search input in top area *(search present when warranted per §7.4)*.
- Drafts section at bottom if any.

### State 5 — Filter row expanded
- Customer taps the collapsed filter chip.
- Row expands showing available filter options (§7.1).
- Tapping an option collapses the row and updates the grid.
- Tapping outside the row also collapses it.

### State 6 — Filter active
- Grid narrows to matching pieces.
- Collapsed chip shows the active filter's name.
- Sort still applies within the filter.

### State 7 — Search active *(post-search-reveal)*
- Customer taps search input.
- Grid narrows to matching results (debounced, semantic per §7.4).
- Filter and sort remain active alongside search.

### State 8 — Tidy-up mode (post-reveal, Mode B+ with 20+ pieces or ask-triggered)
- Long-press / Shift-click / **[Tidy up]** affordance enters mode.
- Cards gain checkbox overlays.
- Bottom action bar: **[Archive]** / **[Move to a series]** (/ [Restore] if archived filter active) / **[Done]**.
- Selected count shown in bar.
- Tapping cards in this mode toggles selection, not open.

### State 9 — Card tap (all modes, not in tidy-up)
- Full card is tappable.
- Transition to Piece Detail via lateral slide (visual system pass to decide; see §13).
- Library state fully preserved for return.

### State 10 — Draft card [Continue →] tap
- Direct transition to Studio at the draft's state, bypassing Piece Detail.
- Library state preserved.

### State 11 — Draft card body tap
- Standard card behavior — opens Piece Detail for inspection.

### State 12 — Card hover (desktop)
- Subtle elevation + border-color shift.
- Video cards: playback controls become visible on the poster (play, expand, duration).
- Text cards: subtle *Read the whole thing →* highlight.
- No content starts playing on hover.

### State 13 — Draft card with background production
- Card shows the plan or partial media as it currently exists.
- Subtle *Still being finished* indicator.
- [Continue →] affordance still present; tapping opens Studio at the in-progress state.

### State 14 — Milestone callout visible
- Quiet callout appears near the top of Library the first time it's opened after a milestone is triggered (§9).
- Small × dismisses; scrolling past also dismisses.
- Dismissed callouts don't reappear in the same session.

### State 15 — Popular introduction *(before the reveal has fired)*
- Not a Library state directly; Alex introduces conversationally on Home first (§8.1).
- Once accepted, the *Popular* filter and sort become available in Library.

### State 16 — Loading
- Layout renders with **skeleton cards** — muted placeholders in the grid.
- Filter chip and search render immediately (interactive after ~200ms).
- Skeleton cards fade to real content as pieces load, batched (not one-by-one).
- **No spinner. Ever.**

### State 17 — Error (Library fetch failed)
- Empty grid area with soft message from Alex-voice:
  - *"I'm having trouble loading your work right now. Try again in a moment."*
  - **[Try again]** below the message.
- Filter and search remain interactive; error is scoped to the grid.

### State 18 — Error (individual card failed to render)
- Card shows a soft placeholder: *"Couldn't load this one."* + **[Try again]** text link scoped to the card.
- Other cards render normally.

### State 19 — Session lost
- Silent redirect to `/sign-in` with `from` preserved.
- On return, Library state (filter/sort/search/scroll) restored to pre-loss values within the session; across sessions, default state applies.

## 12. Copy library

### 12.1 Screen headings

- Default: *"What you've made"*

- *Recent* (default)
- *Popular* (post-reveal)

### 12.7 Search

- Placeholder: *"Search your work"*

- Filter yields zero: *"Nothing here yet."* + optional *"Try clearing the filter."* text link.

### 12.11 Milestone callouts

- First piece: *"Your first piece."*
- Tenth piece: *"Your tenth piece."*
- First series completed: *"Your first series."*
- A year ago today: *"A year ago today: your grand opening video."* (contextual to the specific piece)

### 12.12 Popular introduction (Alex conversational)

- Actions: **[Yes, show me]** / [Not right now]

### 12.13 Tidy-up introduction (Alex conversational)

## 13. Motion & interaction notes

### 13.1 Library open (from nav)

- Layout renders immediately with skeleton cards.
- Real cards fade in in batches as data loads (~200ms per batch of 6–9 cards).
- Filter chip and search render instantly.
- No entrance animation for the screen heading.

### 13.2 Return from Piece Detail

- Library restores exactly as left (§10).
- If a card was tapped and Piece Detail slid in laterally, return slides the opposite direction over ~400ms.
- Scroll position preserved without visible correction.
- Focus returns to the previously tapped card (for keyboard users).

### 13.3 Filter row expand

- Tap on the collapsed filter chip.
- Row expands over ~250ms revealing filter options.
- Small caret rotates.
- Tap on an option collapses the row over ~200ms; grid updates immediately after collapse begins.

### 13.4 Card interactions

- **Tap:** transition to Piece Detail (~400ms lateral slide).
- **Hover (desktop):** subtle elevation + border shift over ~150ms.
- **Long-press (mobile, post-tidy-up-reveal):** enters tidy-up mode; card gains check overlay.
- **[Continue →] tap on draft card:** direct transition to Studio (~400ms wipe).

### 13.5 Sort control

- Tap opens compact picker (dropdown on desktop, bottom sheet on mobile) over ~200ms.
- Selection dismisses picker; grid updates.

### 13.6 Search interactions

- Typing: debounced fetch after ~300ms pause.
- Results fade in over ~200ms; non-matching cards fade out over ~200ms.
- Clear affordance appears once input has content.

### 13.7 Tidy-up mode

- Entry: card gains check overlay over ~150ms.
- Bottom action bar slides up over ~300ms.
- Exit: action bar slides down; check overlays fade.

### 13.8 Milestone callout

- Fades in near the top of Library over ~400ms the first time Library is opened after the milestone triggers.
- No pulse, no attention-grabbing motion.
- Dismissal (× tap or scroll past): fades out over ~300ms.

### 13.9 Popular introduction (on Home)

- Alex delivers the introduction as a normal narration line in Home Zone A greeting.
- Inline **[Yes, show me]** / [Not right now] appear beneath.
- Accepting: subtle confirmation in narration (*"Ok — I'll highlight the ones that are working."*); the Library filter appears the next time Library is opened, with a one-time subtle draw-the-eye animation on the filter chip.

### 13.10 Reduced motion

- All fades and slides reduce to instant.
- Card transitions to Piece Detail become instant swaps.
- Milestone callouts appear instantly without fade.

## 14. Responsive behavior

- **Mobile (< 640px):**
  - Single column at all modes.
  - Mode A: cards full-width with padding.
  - Mode B/C: cards slightly denser but still single-column.
  - Filter chip and sort in top area; sort opens as a bottom sheet.
  - Search input full-width when present.
  - Nav rail at the bottom (per HOME_V1).
- **Tablet (640–1024px):**
  - Mode A: single column, cards ~640px max.
  - Mode B/C: 2 columns.
  - Filter row wraps if needed when expanded.
- **Desktop (> 1024px):**
  - Mode A: single column, cards ~640px max, centered.
  - Mode B/C: 3 columns.
  - Filter row single horizontal line when expanded.
  - Sort control opens as compact dropdown.
  - Nav rail at the left.

**Never:** grid layouts wider than 4 columns on any screen. Overly-dense grids compete with the *"gallery of accomplishments"* feel.

## 15. Accessibility notes

- **Focus on Library open:** natural document focus preserved. If arrived via keyboard tab from nav, focus lands on the first interactive element (collapsed filter chip if Mode B+, otherwise first card).
- **Return from Piece Detail:** focus returns to the previously tapped card.
- **Keyboard:**
  - Tab moves through filter chip → sort → search (if present) → cards → drafts section cards.
  - Enter/Space on filter chip expands the row.
  - Enter/Space on a card opens Piece Detail.
  - Enter on draft's [Continue →] opens Studio directly.
  - Escape exits tidy-up mode if active; otherwise does nothing.
- **Screen reader:**
  - Screen heading announced first (*"What you've made"*).
  - Filter chip announces state (*"All, button. Expands filter options."*).
  - Expanded filter options announced sequentially.
  - Search input has clear label association.
  - Each card announces as: *"Your summer sale video, published Tuesday, by Alex, button, opens the piece."*
  - Series card: *"Your summer promotion series, 3 pieces, published this month, button, opens the series."*
  - Draft card: *"Your welcome message, still in progress, last worked on Tuesday, button, opens the draft. Continue button also available."*
  - Milestone callout: *"Your first piece."* announced via `aria-live="polite"` when it appears.
  - Tidy-up mode entry announced via `aria-live`: *"Tidy-up active. [N] selected."*
- **`prefers-reduced-motion`:** all fades and slides reduce to instant.
- **Contrast:** card metadata meets AA. Filter chip active state meets AA and does not rely on color alone (also uses fill weight). Search input meets AA. Milestone callouts meet AA.

## 16. Design constraints & trade-offs made explicit

**Trade-off 1 — Cards render pieces in their native form, not thumbnails.**
Cost: heavier per-card render, more bandwidth. Benefit: the customer sees what they built. Library feels like a gallery rather than a file browser.

**Trade-off 2 — Cards do not contain editing controls.**
Library invites; Piece Detail immerses. Some products merge these — every card has scrub bars, chapter navigation, quick-edit menus. We keep Library about looking; Piece Detail is about doing.

**Trade-off 3 — Filters collapse by default.**
Some customers expect always-visible controls. We prefer the cleaner gallery look; the collapsed affordance is one tap away.

**Trade-off 4 — Search reveals on need, not on count.**
Requires engineering to instrument scroll-behavior signals or accept the piece-count heuristic. Justified because a fixed count is arbitrary and violates IA-10.

**Trade-off 5 — Semantic search is a design commitment, not a nice-to-have.**
Requires embeddings-based search or equivalent. Justified because the customer's mental model uses concepts, not keywords, and literal-string search would frustrate.

**Trade-off 6 — Customer-vocabulary categories are dynamic.**
Requires an emergent-vocabulary extraction system. Justified because forcing generic categories (*Videos / Posts / Audio*) onto every customer breaks the intimacy of the product.

**Trade-off 7 — *Popular* introduced by Alex first, not surfaced silently.**
Some products just show a "top performers" tab. We introduce it conversationally to preserve the *"the thing my teammate suggested"* framing (IA-10). If the customer declines, we don't surface it.

**Trade-off 8 — Tidy-up mode uses respectful framing, not admin framing.**
Cost: less immediate discoverability compared to a plain *"Select"* button. Benefit: consistent with the pride principle.

**Trade-off 9 — No *Discard* in batch mode; only Archive.**
Delete exists in Piece Detail for individual pieces. Batch operations respect the collection.

**Trade-off 10 — Sort has only two options.**
Some products expose 5+ sort options (by name, by size, by teammate, by date, etc.). We keep it to two because most sorts are irrelevant to a customer looking at their body of work.

**Trade-off 11 — No folder tree, ever.**
Filters + series + search + tidy-up cover the organizational needs. Folders are users maintaining metadata for software. Series are software organizing work for users.

**Trade-off 12 — Milestones are quiet.**
No confetti, no badges, no counts, no celebrations that feel gamified. Recognition, not achievement.

**Trade-off 13 — Draft [Continue →] is a shortcut, but drafts also open in Piece Detail via card body tap.**
Two paths for the same object. Justified because sometimes the customer knows exactly what they want (continue), and sometimes they want to inspect first (open detail).

**Trade-off 14 — Filter and sort don't persist across sessions.**
Some customers expect memory. We default to *All + Recent* at each session start because Library's first impression each visit should be the customer's full body of work.

## Revision History

**v1 — 2026-08-06.** First canonical version. Written and revised during the same session against operator direction. Major decisions incorporated:

- **Pride principle:** Library is the primary embodiment of Philosophy #6 (added to the Brief during this session). Guiding sentence: *"Scrolling through Library should feel like 'look what I've built.'"*
- **State preservation:** returning from Piece Detail restores Library exactly as the customer left it — filter, sort, search, scroll, tidy-up state.
- **Search revealed on need, not on count:** trigger conditions include piece count, scroll-behavior signal, or customer ask.
- **Semantic search:** matches concepts, not just strings (*"Christmas"* finds *Holiday sale*, *December promotion*, etc.).
- **Screen heading:** *"What you've made"* — reinforces accomplishment.
- **Card metadata simplified:** title + one metadata line + at most one secondary chip. No stacked status.
- **Cards contain no editing controls:** poster + play for video; two paragraphs for text; waveform for audio. No timelines, chapters, or mini-editors.
- **Contextual card personalities:** video / text / audio / series each render distinctly. Library reads as a mixed exhibition.
- **Drafts get direct [Continue →]:** tap continues in Studio bypassing Piece Detail; card body tap opens Piece Detail as before.
- **Popular (not *Best performing*):** introduced conversationally by Alex before appearing in Library.
- **Filters collapsed by default:** single chip visible; tap to expand row.
- **Customer-vocabulary categories:** dynamic filter chips emerging from the customer's own naming patterns (a bakery sees *Announcements / Specials / Welcome messages*, a dealership sees *Walkarounds / Financing / Trade-ins*).
- **Sort simplified:** *Recent* / *Popular*. No *Oldest first*.
- **Tidy up (not *Select*):** conversational framing for batch mode.
- **Archive (not *Discard*):** batch actions are respectful of the collection.
- **No folders:** preserved as a firm philosophical decision.
- **Milestone celebrations:** quiet, rare, human. First piece, tenth piece, first series, a year ago today. No gamification.
- **Trade-offs made explicit** across native cards, no editing in Library, filter reveal, semantic search, dynamic categories, conversational reveals, respectful batch framing, no folders, quiet milestones.
