# Home — Screen Design

## v1 — first canonical screen design under PRODUCT_IA_V1

*Anchored in [`PRODUCT_IA_V1_2026-08-06.md`](../PRODUCT_IA_V1_2026-08-06.md) §4. Turns the IA's Home specification into an implementation-informing screen design — visual hierarchy, all states, all copy, motion notes, and the specific design decisions the IA did not resolve.*

*No wireframes. Layout is described in structured text so it can be built without prescribing visual choices that belong to the visual-system track.*

---

## 1. Purpose recap

Home is the control room. The customer arrives here after signing in and after publishing. It answers *"What should I do right now?"* in one sentence and one obviously-primary action. The teammate is present ambiently — the customer feels accompanied, not managed.

Exactly one primary action always exists.

---

## 2. Screen anatomy

Vertical stack of three zones on a single column. Same layout at every breakpoint — only horizontal padding and type-scale change. Home does not have sidebars, top nav, or a footer.

- **Zone A — Presence & greeting** *(top, ~15-20% viewport on desktop)*
- **Zone B — Action set** *(middle, dominant vertical weight)*
- **Zone C — Featured piece** *(bottom, ~30-40% viewport on desktop; **absent for pre-first-publish customers**)*

Navigation is a separate persistent chrome element — see §3.

---

## 3. Navigation

**On Day 1, before the customer's first publish, no navigation exists.** Home fills the viewport. There is nowhere else to go, so no navigation control is shown. The only escape hatch from Home during this pre-publish period is Studio's own top-left "back to Home" affordance (defined in the Studio screen design).

**After the first publish, a persistent navigation appears** containing **Home** and **Library**. After the second teammate is added, **My Team** joins the same navigation. Never more than four items.

Navigation is not laid out as part of Home's vertical zone stack — it is persistent chrome that lives in the app frame, independent of scroll position.

**Placement by breakpoint:**
- **Desktop (> 1024px):** slim left rail, ~48px wide. Quiet visual weight — muted color, small typography, no icons in v1 (icons ship with the visual system track).
- **Mobile (< 640px):** persistent bottom rail, thumb-reach. Same items, laid out horizontally.
- **Tablet (640–1024px):** bottom rail by default, matching mobile. A future visual-system pass may promote to a left rail if it proves comfortable at tablet widths; do not assume it.

The current surface is the only one visually emphasized. Navigation never sits below Zone C.

## 4. Zone-by-zone specification

### 4.1 Zone A — Presence & greeting

**Layout:**
- Teammate face (~64px mobile, ~96px desktop) at left.
- Teammate name + greeting to the right, wrapping as needed.
- Small **[Edit Alex]** affordance below greeting — appears **only** when the customer has exactly one teammate (before the My Team surface exists).

**Typography:**
- Greeting is the largest text on screen after Zone C's media. Should feel like something a person said, not a heading. Body-large weight, generous line-height.
- Teammate name is a quiet byline beneath or beside the greeting — small type, muted. Present at every state (grounds the relationship).

**Face state:**
- Idle: still portrait with subtle life (occasional blink, slight breathing motion). Never lip-sync on Home.
- On hover: no change (Home is not a chat surface).
- On click: nothing while there is only one teammate. Once My Team exists, tapping the face opens that teammate's My Team detail.

### 4.2 Zone B — Action set

**Layout:**
- 3–5 buttons stacked vertically, full-width (bounded to ~480px max on desktop).
- Primary is first (topmost), visually dominant.
- Secondaries follow, quieter.
- *"Ask for something else →"* text link below all buttons, indented slightly, quieter still.

**Button spec:**
- All buttons same height for rhythm.
- Primary: solid fill in a warm brand color, confident typography.
- Secondary: outlined or ghost style, muted.
- Ask link: text-only with right-arrow. Feels like the teammate voice.

**Button-count rules:** min 1 (primary), max 5, typical 3.

**In-progress action variant:** when a piece is being finished in the background (see State 3 in §5), one of the secondary actions carries in-progress treatment — a subtle status indicator (a slow pulse or muted color, no percentage, no spinner) with human-narration copy. Reads as an action, not a status widget. Tapping it opens Studio at the in-progress state.

### 4.3 Zone C — Featured piece

**Layout:**
- Large card containing the piece in its native form.
- Below the card: one soft reveal affordance line — usually a suggested next action (*"Want to make a follow-up? →"*).

**Media rendering by piece form:**

- **Video pieces:** poster frame with an unmistakable play affordance (a prominent play control centered on the poster). **No autoplay.** **No hover-play.** Hover on desktop reveals playback controls and metadata (duration, publish date); it does not begin playback. Tap play to watch inline muted; click title or expand affordance to open a full-screen preview overlay.
- **Text-based pieces** (welcome messages, product announcements, video outlines, written posts, and any other text deliverable): first two paragraphs rendered at readable size, soft fade at the bottom, *"Read the whole thing →"* link. The card is titled with the piece's contextual name (never *"Script"*).
- **Audio/spoken pieces** (voiceovers, live-conversation recordings): title with duration and date, waveform preview, prominent play affordance. Same no-autoplay rule.
- **Series** (a group of related pieces): rendered as **one collection card** with a single lead image (the most recent piece in the series) plus a restrained indication of the group — for example, a small overlapping-thumbnails motif in a corner and a caption like *"3 pieces in this series."* The card does not stack multiple playable items on Home; a customer who wants to see them all taps through to Piece Detail.

**Featured-piece selection logic:**
- Default: most recently published.
- Override if the teammate has a confirmed *"best performing"* preference AND that piece published within the last 30 days.
- Post-publish celebration state: the just-published piece.

## 5. All states enumerated

### State 1 — Fresh customer, first Home visit (celebration variant)
- **A:** face + *"That's live now. Your first piece is out there — and it looks good."*
- **B:** **[Make a follow-up]** / [Start something new] / [See what you published]
- **C:** the just-published piece, rendered as poster frame with explicit play affordance.

Arrival uses the restrained celebration sequence (§7.1). Navigation appears for the first time during this sequence.

### State 2 — Returning, has a draft in progress
- **A:** *"Welcome back. You left off in the middle of your welcome video — want to finish it?"*
- **B:** **[Continue your welcome video]** / [Start something new] / [See everything you've made]
- **C:** most recent publish (if any); else no Zone C.

### State 3 — Returning, has work still being finished in the background
Occurs when the customer left Studio while a render, upload, or async publish was still processing.

- **A:** *"Welcome back. Your summer sale video is still being finished — I'll let you know when it's ready. In the meantime?"*
- **B:** **[Start something new]** (primary) / **[Your summer sale video is still being finished]** (secondary with in-progress treatment; tapping opens Studio at the in-progress state) / [See what you've made] (if the customer has published before)
- **C:** most recent completed publish (if any); else no Zone C.

The in-progress button uses human narration — never percentages, spinners, or technical progress language.

### State 4 — Returning, no drafts, patterns emerging
- **A:** *"Morning, Chris. Looks like you've been posting on Fridays lately — should we keep that up?"*
- **B:** **[Start something new]** / [Make a follow-up to last Friday's post] / [See what you've made]
- **C:** last Friday's piece.

The greeting is phrased as an observation the customer can confirm or correct (see §4.5 — pattern memory confidence).

### State 5 — Returning after quiet week+
- **A:** *"Good to see you back. Nothing's shipped since your product launch video last month — want to make something today?"*
- **B:** **[Start something new]** / [Look at what you published last month]
- **C:** last published piece with a small overline *"your most recent."*

### State 6 — Established user (20+ published, no active draft)
- **A:** *"Welcome back, Chris. Your library's grown to 34 pieces — want to keep going, or explore what's in there?"*
- **B:** **[Start something new]** / [Repurpose your best-performing post] / [See everything you've made]
- **C:** best-performing piece from the past 30 days (once *"best performing"* is a confirmed preference; otherwise most recent).

### State 7 — Pre-onboarding
Customer signed in but hasn't completed onboarding. Redirect to onboarding. Home is not shown.

### State 8 — Loading
- Layout renders immediately with **skeleton content** — face-shaped placeholder in Zone A, three button-shaped placeholders in Zone B, media-card-shaped placeholder in Zone C.
- Skeleton reads as intentional silhouette. Subtle gradient indicates content is coming.
- If any zone's fetch exceeds 800ms, the teammate's face resolves first (cached client-side), then greeting text.
- **No spinner. Ever.** **No** *"Loading…"* text.
- Interactive elements are disabled until their zone hydrates; disabled state is visually distinct but not sad.

### State 9 — Error (backend unreachable)
- **A:** face + *"I'm having trouble connecting right now. Try again in a moment."*
- **B:** single primary button — **[Try again]**. No secondaries.
- **C:** absent. Navigation (if it exists for this customer) is dimmed but still visible.

### State 10 — Error (session lost / expired)
- No visible error state. Silent redirect to `/sign-in` with `from` set so post-login returns here.

### State 11 — Error (partial: greeting OK, featured piece failed)
- A and B render normally.
- C shows a small quiet placeholder: *"Couldn't load your most recent piece right now."* + **[Try again]** text link.
- Customer's next actions are unblocked; the error is a soft note.

## 6. Copy library

### 6.1 Greeting patterns

**Structure:**
- One sentence.
- May include a **context callback** (what happened recently) OR a **pattern reflection** (what the teammate has observed), but not both in the same greeting.
- May include a **soft nudge** implying the primary action, but never repeat the button label.
- Never contains HTTP codes, system status, error messages, or internal terms.

**Templates by moment:**

*Fresh return, <24h since first publish:*
- *"That's live now. Your first piece is out there — and it looks good."*
- *"Good — it's up. Want to make another?"*

*Recent return (1–3 days), has a draft:*
- *"Welcome back. Your welcome video is still open — want to finish it?"*
- *"Morning. You have a draft from yesterday — pick up where you left off?"*

*Recent return, no draft:*
- *"Welcome back. What are we making today?"*
- *"Good to see you. Ready for the next one?"*

*Return with work still processing:*
- *"Welcome back. Your summer sale video is still being finished — I'll let you know when it's ready. In the meantime?"*
- *"Good to see you. Wrapping up your product announcement now — pull up something else while you wait?"*

*Pattern reflection — observation (tentative, seeking confirmation):*
- *"Looks like you've been posting on Fridays lately — should we keep that up?"*
- *"You've kept the last few videos short — should I continue that?"*
- *"Your last few pieces have all been about the summer menu — want to stick with that, or try a different angle?"*

*Pattern reflection — confirmed preference (natural, definite):*
- *"Sticking with the shorter format you like."*
- *"Ready for this week's Friday post?"*
- *"Another summer menu piece coming up — want me to draft it?"*

*Quiet return (7+ days):*
- *"Good to see you back. Nothing's shipped since your product launch video last month — want to make something today?"*
- *"It's been a while. Ready to jump back in?"*

*Established user, no context change:*
- *"Welcome back, Chris. Your library's grown to 34 pieces — want to keep going?"*

**Copy rules:**
- Use the customer's first name only after they've completed one publish. First name comes from account profile, not conversation.
- Never use pronouns for the business unless the customer has provided them naturally.
- Never guess at business specifics not surfaced through onboarding or usage.
- Never mention numbers the customer hasn't earned yet (no *"0 pieces"* empty count).
- Distinguish observations from confirmed preferences (see §4.5). Observations end with a question mark or offer explicit correction; confirmed preferences read as settled fact.

### 6.2 Button copy

**Primary action — direct verb + specific object:**
- **[Start something new]** — safe default.
- **[Continue your \<piece name\>]** — resume specific draft.
- **[Make a follow-up]** — post-publish celebration state.
- **[Try again]** — error state.

**Secondary action copy:**
- [See everything you've made] *(replaces "Open Library")*
- [Make a follow-up to your \<piece name\>]
- [Repurpose your best-performing post] *(only after this preference is confirmed)*
- [Look at what you published last month]
- **[Your \<piece name\> is still being finished]** *(in-progress action variant — see §4.2)*

**Ask link copy:**
- *"Ask for something else →"* — default.

### 6.3 Reveal-line copy (below Zone C)

- After first publish: *"Want to make a follow-up? →"*
- Established user: *"Want me to remix this one into something new? →"*
- Series-suggested: *"These three could work as a series. Want to plan it? →"*

### 6.4 Empty state (no Zone C)

Zone C is simply omitted. **No** *"your work will appear here"* placeholder. That would violate IA-10 (reveals feel inevitable, never announced) by pre-announcing a future surface.

### 6.5 Error copy

- Backend unreachable: *"I'm having trouble connecting right now. Try again in a moment."*
- Featured piece failed: *"Couldn't load your most recent piece right now."*
- Any 5xx from server: *"Something's not right on my end. Try again in a moment?"* + **[Try again]**
- Any 4xx unexpected: same as 5xx (never expose the actual code per IA-6).

**Never used:** *Error, Failed, HTTP codes, "Something went wrong," Stack traces, "Contact support" as first fallback, "brain" or any anthropomorphizing description of the infrastructure.*

## 4.5 Pattern memory — confidence rules

Pattern memory is what turns Home from a control room into a colleague you know. But an observation phrased as fact is worse than no observation — it makes the teammate feel presumptuous or wrong.

Memory therefore has **two states**:

### Observations (provisional)

An observation is what the teammate has noticed but has not yet earned the right to treat as preference. Observations surface **tentatively** in the Home greeting and always leave room for correction:

- *"Looks like you've been posting on Fridays lately — should we keep that up?"*
- *"You've kept the last few videos short — should I continue that?"*
- *"Your last few pieces have all been about the summer menu — want to stick with that?"*

Rules for observation phrasing:
- End with a question mark, or explicitly invite correction.
- Use hedging language: *"looks like,"* *"seems,"* *"lately."*
- Offer the alternative when reasonable: *"…or try a different angle?"*
- Never present as a settled fact.

### Confirmed preferences (definite)

A preference is what the customer has either explicitly confirmed OR demonstrated across multiple sessions with consistent behavior. Confirmed preferences surface as **settled facts** in the greeting:

- *"Sticking with the shorter format you like."*
- *"Ready for this week's Friday post?"*
- *"Another summer menu piece coming up."*

An observation is promoted to a confirmed preference when:
1. The customer explicitly confirms (*"yes, that's right"* / *"keep going"* / a click-through on the observation's implied action).
2. OR the teammate has observed the pattern across multiple separate sessions with consistent behavior (evidence model specified by engineering, tuned to avoid n=1 or same-session false confidence).
3. OR the customer uses the pattern-confirming language themselves in conversation.

### Correction is immediate

When the customer corrects an observation or a confirmed preference:
- *"Actually, I'm going to post daily this week."* → *"Got it — no assumption about Fridays."*
- The teammate acknowledges the correction in the current turn.
- The observation is downgraded or removed on the next Home visit, not persisted as-is with a footnote.
- The customer never sees a *"You corrected me"* record; they just see that the greeting no longer reflects the incorrect pattern.

### Engineering note (design-level requirement)

Engineering can define the specific evidence model — thresholds, decay, session boundaries — later. The screen design requires only that the runtime distinguish observations from confirmed preferences, and expose that distinction to the copy layer so the greeting can select the right phrasing pattern. The design will not accept memory implementations that surface single-observation inferences as confirmed preferences.

## 7. Motion & interaction notes

### 7.1 Arrival — the celebration variant (State 1)

The moment the customer returns to Home after their first publish is the emotional peak of Arc 1. Motion treatment is **restrained**, not theatrical — the customer should feel a moment of *quiet accomplishment*, not a game achievement. Total target: **~1.5–2 seconds**.

Sequence:
1. **T=0:** Layout skeleton visible.
2. **T=~200ms:** Face and greeting fade in together. **No character-by-character typing** — the greeting appears as one complete sentence.
3. **T=~500ms:** Zone B actions settle into place.
4. **T=~900ms:** The just-published work enters Zone C with restrained emphasis — subtle scale-up from 97% to 100% + fade, no bounce.
5. **T=~1400ms:** Navigation materializes for the first time (Home + Library visible). A single soft pulse on the Library item draws the eye once, then stills.
6. **T=~1800ms:** All settled.

Every element is interactive as soon as it renders. The customer can tap anywhere at any time to short-circuit remaining motion.

### 7.2 Arrival — every other state

- Skeleton → real content, 200ms fade per zone as each is ready.
- No entrance animation for buttons — they're just there.
- Zone C fades in.
- Total time-to-interactive: <500ms perceived.

### 7.3 Button interaction

- Hover (desktop): subtle scale to 1.02, background darkens slightly. 150ms ease.
- Press: scale to 0.98 with immediate feedback.
- Click transitions:
  - Primary [Start something new] / [Continue…] → transitions to Studio via full-screen wipe or dissolve. **The teammate's face persists across the transition** — the "coworker walking with you" pattern.
  - Secondary [See everything…] → navigates to Library with a lateral slide. No face persistence needed.
  - [Try again] on error → button pulses once, then fetches; on success, real state fades in over the error state.
  - In-progress action → opens Studio at the in-progress state (Studio's own transition rules apply from there).

### 7.4 Featured piece hover (desktop)

- Hover reveals controls: prominent play button, duration, publish date, expand affordance.
- Hover **does not begin playback.**
- On leaving hover, controls fade back to the resting state (poster frame with subtle play affordance still visible).

### 7.5 Ask link

- Hover: text underline extends left-to-right, arrow moves right ~4px. 200ms ease.
- Click opens the Ask surface as a **sheet**, not a modal:
  - **Mobile:** slides up from the bottom, taking ~40% of viewport, dismissible by drag-down.
  - **Desktop:** anchored side sheet or compact panel connected visually to Home (e.g., slides in from the right or expands from beneath Zone B). The sheet stays adjacent to Home content, not layered over it.
  - **Never a centered modal** unless accessibility or viewport constraints force it.
- Contains a single input, the teammate's face, and space for the teammate's reply. Dismissing returns focus to the Home content where it was.

### 7.6 Navigation

- **First appearance** (the celebration sequence, §7.1): the rail materializes as part of the celebration. On desktop, the left rail slides in from the left over ~400ms; on mobile, the bottom rail slides up from the bottom over the same duration. Library carries a single soft pulse (one only, then still).
- **Subsequent visits:** the rail is simply present; no entrance animation.
- **Adding My Team later:** the new item slides in from the outer end of the rail over ~600ms with a single soft pulse. Never repeats.
- **Current-surface indicator:** subtle underline or dot. No color-flooding of the whole item.

## 8. Responsive behavior

- **Mobile (< 640px):** single-column, generous vertical padding. **Navigation:** persistent bottom rail (thumb reach) once revealed. Zones stack A → B → C. Face at ~64px.
- **Tablet (640–1024px):** same single-column layout, larger padding. **Navigation:** bottom rail. Face at ~80px.
- **Desktop (> 1024px):** single-column layout constrained to a comfortable reading width (~640px content max, centered). **Navigation:** slim left rail (~48px) once revealed. Face at ~96px. Additional whitespace on the sides — **do not** fill it with widgets.

**Never:** two-column layouts on Home. Home is deliberately narrow — the customer's attention is single-file.

**Navigation never occupies vertical space below Zone C.** It is persistent chrome anchored to the viewport, independent of Home's scroll.

## 9. Accessibility notes

- **Keyboard focus:** Home does **not** force focus on load. Natural document focus is preserved. Focus is only restored to a specific element if Home was reached through a flow that explicitly requires focus restoration (e.g., post-login redirect returning to a specific action). Auto-focusing the primary action is disorienting for screen-reader and keyboard users and is prohibited.
- **Focus order (when the customer tabs in):** teammate face (if interactive) → primary action button → other action buttons → Ask link → featured piece (if interactive) → navigation items.
- **Screen reader flow:** greeting text is read after teammate name. Buttons announced with full labels (*"Continue your welcome video, button"*). Featured piece announces as *"Your most recent published piece: \<contextual name\>, followed by playable media."* The in-progress action announces its state: *"Your summer sale video is still being finished, button, opens the work in progress."*
- **`prefers-reduced-motion`:** the celebration sequence (§7.1) plays fades but skips scale-ups. Content still appears in the same order for narrative purpose, but instantly rather than sequenced with delays.
- **Contrast:** greeting text meets **AAA** (7:1) — the emotional anchor should be effortlessly readable. Button text meets AA (4.5:1) minimum. Captions and metadata meet AA.
- **Color independence:** primary/secondary button distinction must not rely on color alone. Weight/fill/outline must also differ. In-progress action state must not rely on color alone (a subtle pulse or icon must accompany).
- **Time-based motion never gates interaction.** Every element is interactive as soon as it renders.

## 10. Design constraints & trade-offs made explicit

**Trade-off 1 — Zone C is absent on Day 1.**
Costs a "richer" first impression. Wins on IA-10 (reveals feel inevitable). Pre-populating with an example or placeholder would announce a future feature.

**Trade-off 2 — No usage stats or metrics dashboard.**
Customers coming from other marketing tools may expect one. Numbers-without-context (impressions, reach) would violate IA-5 (accomplishment over configuration). Metrics surface as *"best performing"* observations inside Library, not as a Home widget.

**Trade-off 3 — Teammate face is always present, even on error.**
On backend-unreachable we still show the teammate delivering the message, but the message itself does not anthropomorphize the infrastructure. Preserves the metaphor without pretending the AI itself is what failed.

**Trade-off 4 — The greeting can vary a lot.**
Six greeting families plus observation/preference variants creates a maintenance surface. Formulaic greetings would break the "colleague you know" feeling. **Copy must be authored, not templated with variable substitution alone.**

**Trade-off 5 — No settings, help, or notifications on Home.**
Some customers will look for them (habit from other apps). We've decided the friction of *"where's settings?"* is smaller than the pollution of adding fifth-noun items. Help surfaces through the Ask link. Settings that matter (teammate adjustments) live in Home Zone A via **[Edit Alex]** or, once revealed, in My Team.

**Trade-off 6 — No autoplay for the featured video.**
Autoplay after publish would give the celebration a stronger immediate emotional hit. We chose the dignified read: the customer *chooses* to play, giving them agency over the moment of re-watching. Consistent with IA-8 (reveals respect flow) and with the calm/warm tone.

**Trade-off 7 — No character-by-character greeting animation.**
Typing animation reads as chatbot theater and delays comprehension. Instant greeting is warmer because it doesn't perform being an AI.

**Trade-off 8 — Pattern memory phrases observations as questions.**
The teammate reflecting *"looks like…"* creates more copy work than treating everything as confirmed fact, but avoids the failure mode of the teammate feeling presumptuous or being confidently wrong.

## 11. Deferred to later work

- **Visual system decisions** (typography scale, color palette, iconography, exact button visuals) — separate track.
- **Voiced greetings** (audio version of Zone A) — accessibility track; not v1.
- **Proactive teammate-generated suggestions** (*"I was thinking about a short reel about your Thursday special. Want to see it?"*) — high-signal capability, held until memory maturity and trust behavior are proven.
- **Analytics for the six experience goals** — engineering track.
- **Localization of the default teammate name** (*"Alex"* is an English-market default) — separate track.

## Revision History

**v1 — 2026-08-06.** First canonical version. Written and revised during the same session against operator direction. Major decisions incorporated:

- **Navigation:** no Day-1 rail. Navigation appears only when Library is revealed (first publish), containing Home + Library from that moment. My Team joins later. Desktop: slim left rail. Mobile/tablet: persistent bottom rail. Navigation is persistent chrome, never laid out below Zone C.
- **Pattern memory:** two-state model — observations (tentative, seeking confirmation) vs. confirmed preferences (settled fact). Design will not accept implementations that surface single-observation inferences as confirmed preferences.
- **Celebration sequence:** restrained, ~1.5–2 seconds. No character-by-character greeting typing. No video autoplay. Poster frame + explicit play affordance.
- **Featured piece:** no hover-play on desktop. Hover reveals controls only. Series render as one collection card with a lead image + restrained group indication, not a stack of playable items.
- **Backend-unreachable copy:** *"I'm having trouble connecting right now. Try again in a moment."* The teammate delivers the message but does not describe the infrastructure as its "brain."
- **Ask surface:** sheet (bottom sheet on mobile, anchored side sheet on desktop), never a centered modal by default.
- **Deliverable names:** always contextual (*"Your welcome message,"* *"Your product announcement,"* *"Your video outline"*). Never generically labeled *"Script."*
- **New state:** background-processing (State 3) — when a piece is still being finished after the customer returns to Home. Shown as a secondary action with in-progress treatment and human-narration copy; other actions remain available.
- **Accessibility:** no auto-focus on the primary action. Preserve natural document focus unless a flow explicitly requires restoration.
- **Trade-offs made explicit** across dashboard omission, autoplay, greeting animation, and memory phrasing.
