# My Team — Screen Design

## v1 — canonical screen design under PRODUCT_IA_V1

*Anchored in [`PRODUCT_IA_V1_2026-08-06.md`](../PRODUCT_IA_V1_2026-08-06.md) §8. The teammates helping the customer show up well. Also the surface where a teammate can be adjusted, a new one added, or an existing one retired, and where a conversation originates once revealed.*

*Sibling to [`HOME_V1_2026-08-06.md`](HOME_V1_2026-08-06.md), [`ONBOARDING_V1_2026-08-06.md`](ONBOARDING_V1_2026-08-06.md), [`STUDIO_V1_2026-08-06.md`](STUDIO_V1_2026-08-06.md), [`LIBRARY_V1_2026-08-06.md`](LIBRARY_V1_2026-08-06.md), [`LIVE_CONVERSATION_V1_2026-08-06.md`](LIVE_CONVERSATION_V1_2026-08-06.md). ## 1. Purpose recap

My Team is where the customer understands, adjusts, and works with the teammates helping them show up well. It answers *"Who is showing up in my marketing, and are they representing me well?"*

The customer should feel: *"These are the teammates helping me show up well."* Not: *"These are configurable presences."*

Philosophy #6 applies: a teammate detail view is a portrait of a creative ally, not a user profile page. Zone framing, action language, and defaults all lean into colleague-ness.

---

## 2. When My Team appears

**My Team nav item does not appear until the customer has added a second teammate.** Before then, the customer's single teammate (Alex, by default) is present ambiently on Home; adjustments happen via a small **[Edit Alex]** affordance in Home Zone A that opens the teammate detail view directly.

Once a second teammate is added:
- My Team nav item appears with a one-time subtle draw-the-eye animation (matching Library's first-reveal pattern).
- Home Zone A's **[Edit Alex]** affordance is replaced with a link to My Team (*"See your team"*).
- The teammate detail view continues to work; it's reachable from both My Team list and (for the current teammate) from Home directly.

**Retirement note:** if the customer later retires teammates back down to one, the My Team nav item **remains** — it was earned. Retirement doesn't un-reveal.

## 3. List view

### 3.1 Anatomy

- **Top area:** small screen heading. Quiet.
- **Main area:** vertical stack of teammate cards.
- **Bottom area:** **[Add another teammate]** affordance — quiet, muted, small.
- **Retired section:** appears above the [Add another teammate] affordance only when retired teammates exist (§9.3). Collapsed by default with a clear affordance to expand.
- **Navigation:** persistent nav frames the surface.

No sidebar. No settings. No admin controls.

### 3.2 Sort order

- **Primary teammate first.** The customer's original teammate (Alex, by default) always leads the list.
- **Remaining active teammates in creation order.** Second-added, third-added, etc.
- **Retired teammates excluded from the main list.** Available via the retired section (§9.3).

**No activity-based reordering.** Familiar teammates should never move around unexpectedly.

Customer-controlled sorting is deferred; if teams grow large enough to need it, that becomes a future consideration.

### 3.3 Teammate card

Each card is a portrait treatment — the teammate leads, metadata supports.

**Structure:**
- **Face** — large and expressive (~120px mobile, ~160px desktop).
  - Small **capability indicator** overlay near the portrait (bottom-right corner), when the conversation capability has been revealed for this teammate: *"Live conversations available."* — quiet, small type. Never a badge.
- **Name** — display type, prominent.
- **One-line role** — the teammate's positioning in the customer's own voice (*"Speaks casually about the bakery to neighborhood locals"* / *"Handles Spanish-speaking customers"* / *"Fronts the commercial side of the business"*).
- **Body-of-work line** — one quiet line, contextual:
  - With work: *"34 pieces made"*
  - No work yet: *"Ready to make their first piece."*

**Forbidden clutter:**
- Multiple stacked metadata lines.
- Analytics numbers on the card.
- File-size, format, or technical metadata.
- Any icon-heavy metadata row.
- Live-call capability as a shared line with body-of-work count.

**Card behavior:**
- Tapping anywhere on the card opens the teammate detail view (§4).
- No inline actions. No quick-adjust menus. No context menus. The card is a warm gateway.
- Hover on desktop: subtle elevation + border shift over ~150ms.

### 3.4 The [Add another teammate] affordance

- Small text-and-icon at the bottom of the list, below the last active card and the retired section (when present).
- Copy: *"Add another teammate →"*
- Tap opens the add-teammate flow (§8).

**Never a "big empty card" invitation.** Adding a teammate is a real choice — the affordance is quiet enough that it doesn't push the customer toward doing so.

## 4. Detail view

The teammate detail view is used from two entry points — Home [Edit Alex] (before My Team exists) and My Team list card tap. Same content, same layout, same behavior.

**This is a work surface for adjusting and working with the teammate.** Philosophy #5 (accomplish work, not configure software) applies with force; Philosophy #6 (pride not inventory) shapes the framing.

### 4.1 Anatomy

- **Top area:** back affordance (context-aware — **[Back to Home]** if reached from Home, **[Back to your team]** if from My Team list). Small persistent status if relevant: *"Adjusted just now"* fades after a moment.
- **Main area:** three sections in continuous scroll — **How \<teammate name\> shows up** (§5), **What \<teammate name\> knows** (§6), **What \<teammate name\> has made** (§7). Section 7 also contains a **Recent conversations** subsection (§7.3) when conversation transcripts exist for this teammate.
- **Primary action:** persistent, floating **[Make something with \<teammate name\>]** button, fixed to the viewport. See §4.4 for behavior during focused adjustment flows.
- **Bottom area:** the retire affordance (§9) — quiet, well below the last section.

### 4.2 Long-scroll layout on all breakpoints

- **Desktop:** long-scroll layout with clear section separations. Persistent [Make something with \<name\>] fixed at the bottom-right of the viewport.
- **Mobile:** long-scroll layout with a **compact sticky section navigator** at the top (below the back affordance). Three labels — **How \<name\> shows up** / **What \<name\> knows** / **What \<name\> has made** — tapping scrolls to the section.
- **Tablet:** desktop-style long-scroll; section navigator not sticky unless viewport height is constrained.

**No tabs anywhere.** Tabs risk making the teammate feel like three administrative records. The three sections are part of one coherent teammate profile.

### 4.3 Section headings use the teammate's name

- **How Alex shows up** (or *María* or whichever teammate)
- **What Alex knows**
- **What Alex has made**

Generic pronouns (*"they"* / *"them"*) are not used in section headings. The teammate's name grounds every heading in the specific person the customer is working with.

### 4.4 Primary action behavior

- Persistent **[Make something with \<name\>]** floats fixed to the viewport (bottom-right on desktop, above the bottom nav on mobile).
- Tapping opens Studio pre-scoped to this teammate.
- **The primary action is hidden or suppressed during focused adjustment flows** (§5.4 opening "Adjust how Alex shows up"; §6.6 knowledge correction; §9 retirement confirmation). During these focused flows, the adjustment surface's own confirmation action becomes the sole obvious primary action.
- On exit from a focused adjustment flow, the persistent primary action fades back in.

## 5. How \<teammate name\> shows up

The section formerly framed as "Presence." Reframed around one clear question: *how does this teammate show up in the world?*

### 5.1 Default view — the cohesive preview

The default detail view presents the teammate as a colleague, not as a set of settings. Elements:

- **Portrait** — the teammate's face at generous size (~240px on desktop, ~160px on mobile). Idle with subtle life (blink, breath).
- **Voice sample** — a small player with **[Play sample]** control. When played, the teammate says the standard voice-comparison line (§5.3).
- **One-sentence style description** — a natural sentence describing how the teammate sounds, written in third person about them: *"Alex sounds warm, conversational, and quietly confident."*
- **Name** — display type, prominent, near the portrait.

**One primary adjustment affordance:**

- **[Adjust how Alex shows up]** — a single button that opens a focused adjustment surface with face, voice, name, and personality controls together (§5.4).

**Not simultaneously displayed on the default view:**
- Face regeneration controls.
- Voice change picker.
- Name-edit affordance.
- Personality-adjust dialogue.

These are all reached through the single adjustment affordance. The default view is clean.

### 5.2 Style description sentence

The one-sentence description is generated from the teammate's personality attributes but rendered as a natural sentence. Examples:

- *"Alex sounds warm, conversational, and quietly confident."*
- *"María sounds bright, friendly, and comfortable in Spanish."*
- *"Jordan sounds direct, professional, and a little dry."*

Rules:
- Written in third person about the teammate.
- Concrete adjectives, not marketing-speak.
- Never a bullet list, never chips, never single-word tags.
- Updated when personality adjustments are confirmed (§5.4).

### 5.3 Voice sample line

The standard voice-comparison line, identical across all voices for direct comparison:

> *"Hi, it's good to meet you. Let me show you what we can do."*

This line offers enough vocal range to compare warmth, pacing, and clarity. Same line used everywhere a voice is sampled — the teammate detail view, the voice-change surface, the add-teammate voice selection.

### 5.4 The focused adjustment surface

Opened by tapping **[Adjust how Alex shows up]**. Presents all four adjustments in one focused surface.

- **Mobile:** full-screen surface with a top **[Done]** affordance.
- **Desktop:** anchored side panel (slides in from right) with a top **[Done]** affordance.

**Sections within the surface** (long-scroll layout, no tabs):

- **Face** — current portrait + **[Try a different face]** (§5.5).
- **Voice** — current voice with **[Play sample]** + **[Change voice]** (§5.6).
- **Name** — current name, inline-editable.
- **Personality** — current one-sentence description + **[Adjust how Alex sounds]** (§5.7).

**Primary action inside the focused surface:** **[Done]** — closes the surface, returns to the teammate detail view. Any adjustments made are already committed (autosave).

During the focused surface, the persistent **[Make something with Alex]** action on the teammate detail view is hidden (§4.4).

### 5.5 Face adjustment — preview before commit

Face changes do **not** commit immediately on selection. The customer needs to compare the new face in context before replacing a familiar teammate.

**Flow:**
1. Tap **[Try a different face]** — opens a face-options sub-surface with 3–6 alternatives generated for this teammate (respecting the teammate's role and audience).
2. Select an option — the option expands into a **larger preview** showing the face at teammate-portrait size, alongside the teammate's name and one-line role for context.
3. Three actions on the preview:
   - **[Use this face]** — commits the change.
   - **[Keep looking]** — returns to the options grid.
   - **[Keep the current face]** — closes the sub-surface without changing anything.
4. After a commit, a temporary **[Undo face change]** affordance appears at the top of the teammate detail view for ~30 seconds.

**Regenerating options:**

If the initial 3–6 options don't feel right, the customer can request more. But regeneration is not a random loop.

- After the first rejected batch, Alex asks: *"What feels off about these?"*
- Options presented as quick-reply chips:
  - **[More approachable]**
  - **[Older]**
  - **[More energetic]**
  - **[Less polished]**
  - **[Younger]**
  - **[Something else]** (opens free-text input)
- Alex generates the next batch from the customer's steer, not randomly.
- The steer chips are contextual — the specific set may vary depending on what Alex thinks might be off.

This makes face selection a collaboration, not a slot-machine loop.

### 5.6 Voice adjustment — preview before commit

Same recoverability principle as face changes.

**Flow:**
1. Tap **[Change voice]** — opens a voice-options sub-surface with 3–6 alternative voices.
2. Each voice has **[Play sample]** using the standard sample line (§5.3).
3. Select a voice — the voice preview shows the teammate's name and role alongside a larger play affordance so the customer can hear the voice in context.
4. Three actions on the preview:
   - **[Use this voice]** — commits.
   - **[Keep looking]** — returns to the options.
   - **[Keep the current voice]** — closes without changing.
5. After commit: **[Undo voice change]** temporary affordance for ~30 seconds.

Regenerating voices follows the same steer-driven pattern as faces (§5.5) when applicable — though voice pools are typically curated rather than generated, so steer chips may point toward *"warmer,"* *"more energetic,"* *"more measured,"* etc.

### 5.7 Personality adjustment

Personality is rendered as the one-sentence description (§5.2). Adjustment happens conversationally.

**Flow:**
- Tap **[Adjust how Alex sounds]** — opens an inline dialogue within the focused adjustment surface.
- Alex asks: *"What should I sound more or less like?"*
- Customer types a response (e.g., *"more energy, less formality"*).
- Alex acknowledges: *"Got it — more energy, less formality. I'll adjust."*
- The one-sentence description updates immediately after the adjustment is confirmed. Customer sees the new description as they return to the surface.

### 5.8 Live-call capability

If the realtime avatar reveal has fired for this teammate, a small indicator appears near the portrait on the default detail view (*"Live conversations available"*) — not treated as generic metadata.

A conversation entry action lives in the teammate detail view but distinct from Zone A's adjustment controls (§10 for placement and language).

## 6. What \<teammate name\> knows

The section formerly framed as "What they know." Reframed with teammate-specific naming and reworked to distinguish factual knowledge from wording, enduring facts from temporary context, and origin from certainty.

### 6.1 Structure

Knowledge is organized into three natural groupings. The customer sees them as visually separated sections within "What Alex knows":

- **About your business** — enduring facts. Location, offerings, positioning, target customers.
- **What matters right now** — temporary context. Current promotions, launches, seasonal focus, near-term events. Time-bound (§6.5).
- **How Alex should represent you** — tone and style preferences. Voice, angle, formality, what to lead with.

These groupings are visible to the customer as section labels, but no toggles or configuration lives here — just the knowledge itself, presented under its natural grouping.

### 6.2 Rendering — first-person prose with origin

Each knowledge statement is rendered in first-person present tense in the teammate's voice, paired with a quiet origin treatment.

**Format:**
```
"I know your bakery is in the West Loop."
   ↳ From what you told me                            (collapsed by default)
```

Origin categories map to natural language:

- **Explicitly told:** *"From what you told me"*
- **Read from supplied material:** *"From your summer menu"* / *"From your product catalog"* / *"From what you shared"*
- **Inferred:** *"My understanding"*

**Rules:**
- Every statement has an origin.
- Origins are visually quiet (small type, subdued color) — the statement dominates.
- Origins can be collapsed by default — a small chevron reveals them on tap or hover; a customer setting could persist expansion, but default is collapsed.
- Origin language never exposes implementation (no *"vector match confidence 0.87"*, no *"chunk 42"*, no filenames).
- The three origin categories should be visually distinguishable enough that a customer can distinguish them at a glance — for example, subtle icon variation or subtle color variation (visual system decides the exact treatment, but the distinction must be perceivable).

**Sentence framing subtly reflects certainty:**
- *"I know…"* → explicitly told or read.
- *"I understand…"* → confidently inferred from strong evidence.
- *"I think…"* → tentative inference, likely to change.

Not all knowledge sounds equally certain. The customer trusts the surface only if it doesn't overclaim.

### 6.3 Adjusting factual knowledge — [Correct this]

Direct click-to-edit is not used for factual knowledge. Factual statements are corrected via a dedicated flow that clarifies the customer is changing the underlying understanding.

**Flow:**
1. Beside each factual statement, a quiet **[Correct this]** affordance.
2. Tap opens a small dialogue: *"What should it be?"*
3. Customer states the correction (e.g., *"We moved to Wicker Park in June."*).
4. Alex rewrites the understanding — the statement updates in place with the corrected fact and its new origin (*"From what you told me"*).
5. If the correction contradicts existing shared knowledge (across multiple teammates), a scope prompt fires (§6.7).
6. Alex confirms in a small acknowledgment: *"Got it — I know the bakery is in Wicker Park now."*

### 6.4 Adjusting wording — direct edit

Wording-only adjustments are appropriate for the *"How Alex should represent you"* section — these are tone/style preferences, not facts.

**Behavior:**
- Click a wording statement to edit inline.
- Autosave applies.
- No scope prompt (tone/style is teammate-specific).

The system's design-level requirement: distinguish factual statements from wording statements. Engineering tunes classification; the design guarantees the two receive different affordances.

### 6.5 Temporary knowledge — time-bound context

Statements in the *"What matters right now"* section are time-bound. They shouldn't remain indefinitely.

**Design behavior:**
- Time-bound context statements carry an internal expected-relevance window (design-level: engineering defines specifics per category — a launch window, a seasonal promotion, an event).
- When the window is exceeded, Alex proactively asks on a subsequent Home visit or during a natural moment:

> *"Your summer menu note may be out of date. Should I remove it or update it?"*

- Two inline actions: **[Remove]** / **[Update]** (which opens a small dialogue for the new information).
- The customer may also simply say *"still relevant"* and the statement's window resets.

Never surfaced as a *"data expiration"* alert. Always in the teammate's voice.

### 6.6 Adding new knowledge — [Teach Alex something new]

At the bottom of the knowledge sections, a quiet affordance: **[Teach Alex something new →]**.

Tapping presents two paths:

- **[Tell Alex]** — opens a conversational input: *"What should I know?"* Customer types freely.
- **[Share a document]** — opens a file input with a natural-language prompt: *"Share a menu, service list, guide, or anything else Alex should understand."*

**Document upload behavior:**
- Accepts common document formats (PDF, images with text, common office formats).
- After upload, Alex processes and surfaces resulting knowledge as prose in the appropriate section:

> *"I read your product catalog. Here's what I understood: your fall menu includes six new items, focused on comfort flavors. The maple bourbon pie is your featured item."*

- Origin treatment: *"From your product catalog"* — filename becomes the natural-language reference.
- The uploaded file itself is never surfaced as a filename in the primary UI. Customers who want to see what they uploaded can access a quiet **[See sources]** subsection at the bottom of the knowledge section — a list of prose descriptions of what was shared (*"Your product catalog, shared last Tuesday"*), not a file browser.

### 6.7 Cross-teammate scope prompts

When a customer changes a business-wide fact (facts in the *"About your business"* section), Alex asks whether the change should propagate to the whole team.

**Prompt:**

> *"Should I update this for your whole team, or only Alex?"*
> Actions: **[Whole team]** / **[Only Alex]**

The prompt fires only for factual changes in the *"About your business"* section. Preferences in *"How Alex should represent you"* are teammate-specific by default (never propagated). Temporary context in *"What matters right now"* prompts scope similarly to factual knowledge.

**Never called "shared knowledge" or "private knowledge"** in customer-facing copy. The prompt just asks about scope.

### 6.8 Knowledge conflicts — customer wins by default

When a correction contradicts existing knowledge from a different origin (e.g., customer corrects a fact previously inferred from a shared document), the customer's current statement usually wins.

**Copy:**

> *"That's different from what I learned from your catalog. I'll use what you just told me. Should I update the rest of your team too?"*

The system doesn't ask the customer to choose whom to trust — it defaults to their current statement and only asks about propagation.

**Only ask for clarification when the customer's new statement is itself ambiguous** (e.g., contradicts *itself* in a way Alex can't parse). Those cases are rare.

## 7. What \<teammate name\> has made

The section formerly framed as "Their body of work." Reframed with teammate-specific naming and reworked as a curated preview, not a mini-library.

### 7.1 Curated preview

Show 3–6 pieces — not nine. The section's purpose is to reinforce identity and provide a path to Library, not to become another browsing surface.

**Selection logic:**
- **Most recent 2–3 pieces** across all formats.
- **1 meaningful highlight** if available (popular piece, first piece, or a piece the customer has referenced recently).
- Total: 3–6 pieces depending on availability.

Cards use the compact variant of Library's card design (§4 of LIBRARY_V1) — same contextual rendering per media type.

### 7.2 See more affordance

Below the curated pieces:

> **[See everything Alex has made →]**

Opens Library with the teammate filter pre-active (per LIBRARY_V1 §7.1).

### 7.3 Recent conversations — separate subsection

Conversation transcripts are **not** automatically treated as creative work. A conversation transcript is evidence of an interaction, not necessarily a piece the customer proudly made.

When conversations exist for this teammate, a separate quiet subsection appears below "What Alex has made":

**Recent conversations**

Each entry shows:
- **Customer or session label** — the counterparty's name or a session identifier (*"Call with Jamie"* / *"Call with an anonymous visitor"*).
- **Date** — natural phrasing (*"Tuesday"* / *"3 weeks ago"*).
- **Duration** — *"8 minutes"*.
- **Short summary** — one-line summary of the conversation topic (*"Talked through the summer menu; helped Jamie decide on the lemon gelato."*).

Entries do not mix into "What Alex has made" unless the customer explicitly turns a transcript into a reusable piece (a capability that lives in Piece Detail and Live Conversation — spec'd there).

**Below the subsection:**
> **[See all conversations with Alex →]**

Opens a scoped view (also part of Live Conversation spec).

If no conversations exist, the subsection doesn't render.

### 7.4 Empty state — no work yet

If the teammate hasn't made anything yet (freshly added, or all pieces retired):

- Quiet message: *"Ready to make the first piece with Alex."*
- Inline **[Make something with Alex]** — same primary action, placed contextually.

No placeholder cards, no "start creating" callouts.

## 8. Adding a teammate

Triggered by:
- **[Add another teammate]** on My Team list.
- **[Add another teammate]** from the Home [Edit Alex] flow (before My Team exists).
- Ask-triggered or usage-triggered reveal (per IA §9.2).

### 8.1 Flow shape

A short conversation with Alex, mirroring Onboarding's pattern. Full-screen layout, quiet **[Leave for now]** escape affordance in the top-right.

### 8.2 Question sequence

**Q1 — Purpose:**
> *"What do you want this teammate to help with?"*
- Free-text.
- Alex may offer example directions as quiet quick-reply chips: *"Reach a specific audience"* / *"Present a different part of the business"* / *"Use a different style for certain customers"* / *"Handle conversations."*
- Customer types or taps a chip.

**Q2 — Audience:**
> *"Who should they connect with?"*
- Free-text, informed by Q1's answer.
- If Q1 mentioned an audience directly (e.g., *"Spanish-speaking customers"*), Alex may skip Q2 and proceed to Q3 with a brief acknowledgment.

**Q3 — Focus:**
> *"Is there anything this teammate should focus on?"*
- Free-text.
- Not comparative ("*that Alex doesn't"*).
- Customer answers freely; empty submission accepted.

**Q4 — Visual direction:**
> *"How should they come across?"*
- Quick-reply chips: **[Approachable]** / **[Polished]** / **[Energetic]** / **[Calm]** / **[Describe it yourself]** (opens free-text).
- Customer taps a chip or types freely.
- This step materially improves face generation quality without becoming a configuration wizard.

**Q5 — Naming:**
> *"What should we call them?"*
- Free-text input.
- Customer types the teammate's name.
- **No culturally inferred suggestions.** Alex does not propose *"María"* because the customer said *"Spanish-speaking customers."*
- If the customer asks for name help (*"suggest some names"* or an inline **[Suggest a name]** affordance), Alex offers a diverse, deliberately neutral set of suggestions (short, easy to say aloud, culturally varied but not inferred from any Q1–Q4 answer).

### 8.3 [Use a good starting point] — the skip option

At Q4 (visual direction), a **[Use a good starting point]** action is offered alongside the direction chips. Tapping it lets Alex select sensible defaults for face and voice; the customer proceeds directly to Q5 (naming). Adjustments can be made anytime later from the teammate detail view.

This keeps the add-teammate flow aligned with Day-1 onboarding philosophy — no requirement to make aesthetic choices before the teammate exists.

### 8.4 Face selection (unless skipped)

- 3–6 face options generated based on Q4's visual direction and the teammate's role.
- Preview flow matches §5.5 (select → larger preview → commit).
- Regeneration follows the steer-driven pattern from §5.5.
- Alternatively, the customer can tap **[Use a good starting point]** here too to skip.

### 8.5 Voice selection (unless skipped)

- 3–6 voice options, each with **[Play sample]** using the standard line (§5.3).
- Preview flow matches §5.6.
- **[Use a good starting point]** available.

### 8.6 Completion

Once name is set (and face/voice either selected or defaulted):

- Alex confirms: *"\<Name\>'s ready. Want to make something with them now, or come back later?"*
- Actions: **[Make something with \<name\>]** (primary — opens Studio pre-scoped) / **[See your team]** (secondary — returns to My Team list with the new teammate visible).

### 8.7 Persistence

Add-teammate drafts persist durably. If the customer leaves the flow (via [Leave for now], tab close, session loss), they can resume where they left off.

- Persistence lasts **at least 30 days** without warning.
- After 30 days of inactivity: Alex offers on next visit: *"You started adding a new teammate a while back — want to pick it up, or start fresh?"* — no silent discard.
- If the customer explicitly discards the draft (via a **[Discard]** action in the flow's escape sheet), it's cleared immediately.

## 9. Retiring a teammate

Retiring is respectful — a teammate is not deleted, they're set aside.

### 9.1 Affordance

- Small **[Retire this teammate]** text link at the bottom of the teammate detail view, well below the last section.
- Muted color, small type. Quiet.

### 9.2 Confirmation

Tap opens a small sheet (bottom sheet on mobile, anchored side sheet on desktop):

- Heading: *"Retire \<teammate name\>?"*
- Body: *"They'll no longer show up in your team, and you can't make new pieces with them. Everything they've made stays saved."*
- Actions: **[Retire]** (primary, but muted — not a warm color) / **[Not now]** (secondary)

During confirmation, the persistent [Make something with \<name\>] primary action is hidden (§4.4).

### 9.3 After retirement — visible completion and undo

Retirement completes with a visible notice, not silent disappearance:

- **Notice:** appears at the top of the My Team list (where the customer lands after confirming retirement).
- **Copy:** *"Alex has been retired. Everything they made is still here."*
- **Actions on the notice:**
  - **[Undo]** — restores the teammate immediately.
  - **[View retired teammates]** — expands the retired section (§9.4).
- **Duration:** notice remains visible until dismissed (× affordance) or until the customer navigates away from My Team. Never auto-dismisses within the session.

### 9.4 Retired teammates section — discoverable

When any retired teammates exist, a collapsed section appears in the My Team list above the [Add another teammate] affordance:

- Section heading: *"Retired teammates"* — small, quiet, with an expand caret.
- Collapsed by default; showing the count (*"3 retired"*).
- Tapping expands to show the retired teammates as smaller cards.
- Each retired card has a **[Bring back]** action.

**Never hidden as an obscure footer link.** The section is quiet but findable without scrolling to obscurity.

### 9.5 Bringing back a retired teammate

- Tap **[Bring back]** on a retired card.
- Confirmation is minimal: teammate immediately returns to the active list; a small notice at the top: *"Alex is back."*
- Teammate returns with all their body of work intact.

### 9.6 Preventing retirement of the last teammate

If the customer somehow tries to retire their only active teammate, the retire flow blocks with **neutral interface copy** (not Alex's voice — Alex speaking about their own retention would feel self-preserving):

- **Notice:** *"You need at least one active teammate. Add another teammate before retiring Alex."*
- **Action:** **[Add another teammate]** — opens the add-teammate flow.

## 10. Live call entry

The realtime avatar capability is a **teammate capability**, not a top-level surface (per IA §8.6). It reveals through the pattern described in IA §9.2.

**Once revealed for a given teammate:**
- The teammate detail view gains a conversation entry action, placed near the portrait in the "How Alex shows up" section, adjacent to (not mixed into) the adjustment affordance.
- The card in the My Team list gains the *"Live conversations available"* indicator near the portrait (§3.3).

### 10.1 Entry action

**Copy:** **[Start a conversation with Alex]**

This is unambiguous — the customer is initiating a call with Alex; Alex is participating on the call. It does not read as impersonation.

*(When customer-facing deployment surfaces exist — e.g., a conversation an external customer initiates through a shared link — different copy will apply. Final label is deferred to the customer-facing design pass; a provisional example is **[Have a conversation with Alex]**. That variant is spec'd in the Live Conversation screen. My Team's entry action is always operator-initiated.)*

### 10.2 Transition

Tapping **[Start a conversation with Alex]** transitions to the Live Conversation screen (separate design later). Face persists across the transition.

### 10.3 After a call

On return, if a transcript was captured, it appears in the *Recent conversations* subsection of the teammate detail view (§7.3) — not in *"What Alex has made."*

## 11. All states enumerated

### State 1 — My Team list appears (first visit after second-teammate reveal)
- Reached via the newly-appeared My Team nav item.
- List shows both teammates as portrait cards.
- Primary teammate (Alex) first, second teammate below.
- **[Add another teammate]** at the bottom.
- No modal welcome. No tutorial. The customer just sees their team.

### State 2 — My Team list (2+ teammates)
- Standard list layout.
- Primary teammate first; others in creation order.
- Retired section (§9.4) appears above [Add another teammate] only if retired teammates exist.

### State 3 — Teammate detail view (reached from My Team list card tap)
- Long-scroll layout: How \<name\> shows up / What \<name\> knows / What \<name\> has made / Recent conversations (if any).
- Persistent floating **[Make something with \<name\>]** action visible.
- **[Back to your team]** top-left affordance.
- Mobile: sticky section navigator at top.

### State 4 — Teammate detail view (reached from Home [Edit Alex])
- Same layout as State 3.
- **[Back to Home]** top-left affordance instead.

### State 5 — Focused adjustment surface open ("Adjust how Alex shows up")
- Sheet slides in.
- Persistent [Make something with Alex] hidden (§4.4).
- Face / Voice / Name / Personality sections presented long-scroll within the surface.
- Top **[Done]** to close.

### State 6 — Face selection preview (during focused adjustment or add-teammate)
- Options grid → selection expands to preview → [Use this face] / [Keep looking] / [Keep the current face].
- After commit: **[Undo face change]** appears at top of teammate detail for ~30s.

### State 7 — Face steer prompt after rejected batch
- Alex asks *"What feels off about these?"* with quick-reply chips (**[More approachable]** / **[Older]** / etc.).
- Customer taps or types; Alex generates from the steer.

### State 8 — Voice selection preview
- Same pattern as face: preview before commit, [Undo voice change] temporary affordance.

### State 9 — Personality adjustment dialogue
- Opens within the focused adjustment surface.
- Alex asks *"What should I sound more or less like?"*
- Customer types; Alex acknowledges; description updates immediately.

### State 10 — Knowledge section default view
- Three subsections (About your business / What matters right now / How Alex should represent you).
- Each statement rendered in first-person prose with quiet origin treatment (collapsed by default).
- Adjustment affordances: **[Correct this]** on factual statements; direct-edit on wording statements; **[Teach Alex something new →]** at the bottom.

### State 11 — Correcting a factual statement
- Tap **[Correct this]** → dialogue: *"What should it be?"*
- Customer states correction.
- Alex rewrites; scope prompt (§13) fires if applicable.
- Acknowledgment lands in Alex's voice.

### State 12 — Direct-edit on a wording statement
- Click to place cursor; edit inline; autosave.

### State 13 — Cross-teammate scope prompt
- Fires when a factual change in "About your business" affects business-wide truth.
- Prompt: *"Should I update this for your whole team, or only Alex?"* + **[Whole team]** / **[Only Alex]**.

### State 14 — Knowledge conflict from correction vs. source
- Alex reconciles: *"That's different from what I learned from your catalog. I'll use what you just told me. Should I update the rest of your team too?"* + scope actions.

### State 15 — [Teach Alex something new] — Tell Alex
- Dialogue: *"What should I know?"*
- Customer types freely.
- Alex acknowledges and integrates into the appropriate section.

### State 16 — [Teach Alex something new] — Share a document
- File input with prompt: *"Share a menu, service list, guide, or anything else Alex should understand."*
- After upload, Alex processes and surfaces resulting knowledge as prose with the document as origin.

### State 17 — [See sources]
- Quiet affordance at the bottom of the knowledge section.
- Tap opens a list of prose descriptions of shared materials (*"Your product catalog, shared last Tuesday"*), not filenames.

### State 18 — Temporary knowledge check-in
- On a Home visit past the expected-relevance window, Alex asks: *"Your summer menu note may be out of date. Should I remove it or update it?"*
- Inline actions: **[Remove]** / **[Update]** (opens dialogue).

### State 19 — Body of work section
- 3–6 pieces rendered as compact Library cards.
- **[See everything Alex has made →]** below.
- Empty state: *"Ready to make the first piece with Alex."* + inline [Make something with Alex].

### State 20 — Recent conversations subsection
- Appears when transcripts exist for this teammate.
- Each entry: customer/session label, date, duration, short summary.
- **[See all conversations with Alex →]** below.

### State 21 — Conversation entry
- **[Start a conversation with Alex]** action visible near the portrait when the capability is revealed.
- Tap transitions to Live Conversation screen.

### State 22 — Add-teammate flow
- Full-screen conversation, mirroring Onboarding.
- Sequence: Purpose → Audience → Focus → Visual direction → Face → Voice → Naming.
- **[Use a good starting point]** available at visual direction, face, and voice steps.
- Persistence: at least 30 days.

### State 23 — Add-teammate resume
- Returning within 30 days: flow resumes at the last completed question with prior answers preserved.
- After 30 days: Alex offers to resume or start fresh; no silent discard.

### State 24 — Add-teammate completion
- Confirmation: *"\<Name\>'s ready. Want to make something with them now, or come back later?"* + **[Make something with \<name\>]** / **[See your team]**.

### State 25 — Retirement confirmation
- Sheet with heading, body, and actions.
- Persistent [Make something with \<name\>] hidden.

### State 26 — Retirement completed notice
- Notice at the top of My Team list: *"Alex has been retired. Everything they made is still here."* + **[Undo]** / **[View retired teammates]**.
- Persists until dismissed or navigation away.

### State 27 — Retired teammates section expanded
- Collapsed section (§9.4) expanded on tap.
- Retired teammates shown as smaller cards with **[Bring back]** per teammate.

### State 28 — Bring back a retired teammate
- Tap **[Bring back]** → teammate returns to active list.
- Notice at top: *"Alex is back."*

### State 29 — Preventing retirement of last teammate
- Neutral interface notice: *"You need at least one active teammate. Add another teammate before retiring Alex."* + **[Add another teammate]**.

### State 30 — Loading (list)
- Skeleton cards render immediately; fade to real content.

### State 31 — Loading (detail)
- Layout renders with placeholders in each section; each hydrates independently.

### State 32 — Error (list fetch failed)
- Empty list area with soft message: *"I'm having trouble loading your team right now. Try again in a moment."* + **[Try again]**.

### State 33 — Error (detail section failed)
- Layout renders with soft placeholder in the failed section: *"Couldn't load this right now."* + **[Try again]** scoped to the section.

### State 34 — Session lost
- Silent redirect to `/sign-in` with `from` preserved.
- On return, restore last teammate viewed and section position (§16).

### State 35 — State preservation on return
- Returning to My Team list restores scroll position and previously-focused card.
- Returning to teammate detail restores previously-visited section (via sticky navigator on mobile) or scroll position (desktop).
- Cross-session persistence per §16.

## 13. Motion & interaction notes

### 13.1 My Team list open

- Layout renders with skeleton cards; real cards fade in over ~200ms as loaded.
- **[Add another teammate]** and retired section (if applicable) fade in with the last card.

### 13.2 Teammate card tap

- Card scales briefly to 0.98 on press.
- Transition to teammate detail via lateral slide (~400ms) on desktop; full-screen slide-up on mobile.

### 13.3 Return to My Team list

- Reverse slide.
- Scroll position and previously-focused card preserved.

### 13.4 Focused adjustment surface

- Slides in over ~300ms (bottom sheet on mobile, side panel on desktop).
- Persistent [Make something with \<name\>] hides with a subtle fade (~200ms) as surface opens.
- Closing the surface reverses; persistent action fades back in.

### 13.5 Face preview flow

- Options grid: 3–6 options fade in over ~300ms as generated.
- Selection: chosen option expands to preview size over ~400ms while other options fade back.
- Commit: preview fades out; teammate detail's portrait updates with subtle blend over ~500ms.
- **[Undo face change]** fades in at top over ~200ms; persists ~30s; fades out.

### 13.6 Face steer prompt

- After first rejected batch: quick-reply chips fade in beneath a warm prompt.
- Steer applied: new batch generates with a brief transitional narration in the surface.

### 13.7 Voice preview flow

- Same pattern as face, with play controls prominent.

### 13.8 Personality adjustment

- Dialogue expands inline within the focused surface.
- After adjustment: description sentence updates on the surface with subtle cross-fade (~400ms).

### 13.9 Knowledge section

- Statements fade in with the section as it hydrates.
- Origin reveal (tap chevron): origin line fades in below statement over ~200ms.
- **[Correct this]** dialogue: opens as inline expansion or small sheet.
- Alex's rewrite: statement updates in place with subtle cross-fade after acknowledgment.

### 13.10 Body of work section

- Compact cards fade in as loaded.
- **[See everything Alex has made →]** transitions to Library with lateral slide.

### 13.11 Live-call subsection

- Fades in with the parent section when entries exist.

### 13.12 Adding a teammate

- Full-screen conversation with fades between questions (matching Onboarding).
- Face options and voice options fade in as generated.
- **[Use a good starting point]** transitions to naming with a brief cross-fade.

### 13.13 Retirement

- Confirmation sheet slides in over ~300ms.
- On confirmation: teammate card fades out from list over ~400ms; remaining cards reflow smoothly; completion notice appears at top.
- **[Undo]** on notice: teammate card fades back in.

### 13.14 Retired section

- Collapsed section fades in below the last active card.
- Expand: section grows to reveal retired cards over ~300ms.

### 13.15 Reduced motion

- All fades and slides reduce to instant.
- Section expansions become instant.
- Sticky navigator on mobile updates position instantly.

- **Mobile (< 640px):**
  - List: single-column stack of cards.
  - Detail: long-scroll with sticky section navigator at top.
  - Persistent [Make something with \<name\>] fixed above bottom nav.
- **Tablet (640–1024px):**
  - List: single column, cards centered.
  - Detail: long-scroll; section navigator may or may not be sticky based on viewport height.
- **Desktop (> 1024px):**
  - List: single-column, cards centered, generous whitespace on sides.
  - Detail: long-scroll with three sections and floating [Make something with \<name\>] fixed at bottom-right.

**Never:** tabs on any breakpoint for the teammate detail view. Sections are part of one coherent profile.

## 15. Accessibility notes

- **Focus on My Team list open:** natural document focus preserved.
- **Focus on teammate detail open:** natural focus; first interactive element is the back affordance.
- **Return from teammate detail to list:** focus returns to the previously-tapped card.
- **Keyboard:**
  - Tab moves through cards → retired section (if present, and includes bring-back actions when expanded) → [Add another teammate].
  - Enter/Space on card opens detail.
  - In detail: Tab moves through section navigator (mobile) → section interactive elements → persistent primary action → back.
  - In focused adjustment surface: Tab through Face / Voice / Name / Personality sub-controls → [Done].
  - Escape in modal sheets dismisses; escape in focused adjustment surface closes it.
- **Screen reader:**
  - List heading announced first (*"Your team"*).
  - Each card announces as: *"María, handles Spanish-speaking customers, 12 pieces made, live conversations available, button, opens the teammate."* (Include *"live conversations available"* only when the capability indicator is present.)
  - Detail: back affordance first, then sticky section navigator (mobile) with each label announced as a link, then section-by-section content.
  - Face: image is decorative; teammate name and role provide context. Never describe physical appearance in alt text.
  - Voice: **[Play sample]** announces as *"Play voice sample, button."* Sample audio is announced as *"Playing voice sample."*
  - Knowledge statements: read in reading order with origin. Correction affordance announces as *"Correct this fact, button."*
  - Scope prompt: announced via `aria-live` when it appears.
  - Retirement notice: announced via `aria-live` with full copy + actions.
  - Sticky section navigator (mobile): each label announced as a link with current-section indicator.
- **`prefers-reduced-motion`:** all fades and slides reduce to instant.
- **Contrast:** teammate name meets AAA (7:1). Card metadata meets AA. Section headings meet AAA. Knowledge statements meet AA at minimum; long-form prose should meet AAA where possible. Retire affordance meets AA even in muted color.

## 16. State preservation

Persistence extends across sessions where the runtime supports it.

**Preserved within-session (memory-cheap):**
- Scroll position on list and detail.
- Focused card / focused section on return.
- Filter/sort settings (though minimal on My Team).

**Preserved across-session:**
- **Last teammate viewed** — if the customer was on a teammate detail when they left, returning to My Team surfaces that teammate first (via a subtle highlight in the list) or opens directly to that teammate's detail if the operator was mid-flow.
- **Mobile section position** — if practical (best-effort).
- **In-progress teammate creation** — at least 30 days per §8.7.
- **Unsaved knowledge correction** — if the customer typed a correction but didn't submit, the draft survives session loss.
- **Face / voice selection flow** — an open selection sub-surface survives session loss up to the same window.

Customers should not be forced back to the list solely because they signed out or closed the browser.

## 17. Design constraints & trade-offs made explicit

**Trade-off 1 — My Team is portrait-shaped, not admin-shaped.**
Larger cards, fewer inline controls, no context menus. Cost: less operational density. Benefit: the customer's team feels like colleagues, not accounts.

**Trade-off 2 — Zone A is reframed as "How Alex shows up" with one primary adjustment.**
Some products expose every adjustment simultaneously. We prefer a cohesive preview + one entry point. Cost: an extra tap to reach face/voice controls. Benefit: the default detail view doesn't feel like an account-preferences form.

**Trade-off 3 — Face/voice changes preview before commit.**
More clicks per change. Justified because a familiar teammate should not be replaceable with a single misclick.

**Trade-off 4 — Face regeneration is steer-driven, not random.**
Requires Alex to interpret free-text or chip steers to influence generation. Justified because random regeneration is a slot-machine loop and produces worse outcomes.

**Trade-off 5 — Voice sample is a curated meaningful line, not "Hi how are you."**
Cost: one voice line to author and cross-produce. Benefit: real vocal-range comparison.

**Trade-off 6 — Personality as sentence, not chips.**
Cost: more copy authoring. Benefit: reads like a description of a colleague, not configuration metadata.

**Trade-off 7 — Knowledge origin surfaced quietly on every statement.**
Cost: more visual detail per statement; runtime must track and expose origin. Benefit: trust — the customer distinguishes what they told Alex from what Alex inferred.

**Trade-off 8 — [Correct this] flow for factual knowledge instead of direct edit.**
Cost: more clicks. Benefit: the customer clearly changes underlying fact rather than editing wording; scope prompts fire cleanly.

**Trade-off 9 — Knowledge structured into three sections.**
Cost: engineering must classify. Benefit: temporary context doesn't become permanent identity.

**Trade-off 10 — Temporary knowledge expires with a check-in.**
Requires runtime to track expected-relevance windows. Justified because summer promotions should not remain in the teammate's identity after fall.

**Trade-off 11 — Scope prompt on cross-teammate changes.**
Some products propagate silently. We ask once. Cost: one interaction per business-fact change. Benefit: customer control over scope.

**Trade-off 12 — Document upload in v1.**
Adds an input method beyond conversational typing. Some products defer file support. Justified because businesses have menus/catalogs/policies and typing every fact is unrealistic.

**Trade-off 13 — Documents never appear as filenames in the primary UI.**
Cost: engineering must present processed knowledge as prose consistently. Benefit: the surface remains prose-based; the customer doesn't manage files.

**Trade-off 14 — Knowledge conflicts default to the customer's current statement.**
The system does not challenge the customer to pick whom to trust. Cost: previously-inferred knowledge may be overwritten aggressively. Benefit: the customer is treated as the authority.

**Trade-off 15 — Body of work is a curated preview (3–6 pieces), not a mini-library.**
Cost: some customers may want more visible pieces on the teammate detail. Justified because Library exists for browsing; My Team's body-of-work section is for identity reinforcement.

**Trade-off 16 — Conversation transcripts live in a separate subsection.**
Some products merge everything into a single work list. We separate transcripts because they're evidence of interactions, not creative work. Cost: two subsections in "What Alex has made" area. Benefit: clarity of identity.

**Trade-off 17 — Add-teammate begins with purpose, not audience.**
The customer may not naturally think in audience segments. Purpose is easier to answer. Justified because a clearer role produces better face/voice/knowledge defaults.

**Trade-off 18 — No culturally inferred name suggestions.**
Requires deliberate restraint in the naming step. Justified as a hard safety and dignity requirement — the system must not couple language or audience with stereotyped identity.

**Trade-off 19 — Add-teammate persistence is 30 days minimum.**
Some products discard drafts after a session. Justified because customer trust in continuity is more valuable than storage savings.

**Trade-off 20 — Retirement completes with a visible notice and undo.**
Some products silently remove. We surface the completion respectfully because retiring a teammate is a meaningful moment.

**Trade-off 21 — Persistent primary action hides during focused adjustment flows.**
Cost: the customer momentarily loses access to [Make something with Alex] while adjusting. Justified because a competing primary action breaks the one-obvious-action rule during focused flows.

**Trade-off 22 — State preserved across sessions.**
Requires backend persistence for last-teammate-viewed, in-progress flows, etc. Justified by continuity — customers shouldn't be reset by tab close.
