# Studio — Screen Design

## v1 — canonical screen design under PRODUCT_IA_V1

*Anchored in [`PRODUCT_IA_V1_2026-08-06.md`](../PRODUCT_IA_V1_2026-08-06.md) §5 and §6. The making surface. The place where a customer's idea becomes a piece they will publish.*

*Sibling to [`HOME_V1_2026-08-06.md`](HOME_V1_2026-08-06.md) and [`ONBOARDING_V1_2026-08-06.md`](ONBOARDING_V1_2026-08-06.md). No wireframes.*

## 1. Purpose recap

Studio is where the customer turns intent into a finished piece with the teammate's continuous collaboration. It should feel like sitting with a colleague who is doing the work in front of you — talking through decisions, welcoming input, but confidently taking the pen.

Exactly one obvious primary action always exists — and its label describes exactly what happens next.

Studio has a **commitment boundary** in the middle of the flow: an explicit moment where the customer commits an editable plan to actual production. Before this boundary, changes are fast; after it, changes may cost time to remake.

---

## 2. Screen anatomy

Studio is **full-screen, edge-to-edge**. The three-zone layout is fixed on desktop; stacked with a persistent header on mobile.

- **Zone L — Teammate presence & narration** *(desktop left, ~25% width; mobile top, compact)*
- **Zone C — The piece (as plan, then as production, then as media)** *(desktop center, ~50% width; mobile main area)*
- **Zone R — Contextual steering + primary action** *(desktop right, ~25% width; mobile bottom drawer, collapsed by default)*

**Persistent chrome:**
- Top-left: **[Back to Home]** — saves and exits Studio. Next to it, a small persistent status indicator: *Saved* / *Saving…* / *Couldn't save — retrying.*
- No top nav, no side nav, no logo. Nothing else competes with the three zones.

## 3. Zone-by-zone specification

### 3.1 Zone L — Teammate presence & narration

**Layout (desktop):**
- Face at top, ~120px, always visible.
- Teammate name beneath the face (quiet byline).
- **Narration stream** below the name, taking the remainder of the vertical space.

**Layout (mobile):**
- Compact header row spanning full width, ~64px tall.
- Face at ~48px on the left; latest narration line to the right.
- Tapping the header expands the narration stream into a dismissible bottom sheet.

**Narration frequency (design-level rule):**

Narration is not a running commentary. New lines land **only when at least one of the following is true:**

- A meaningful decision is made (choice of angle, tone, format, opening).
- A new phase begins (drafting → production, or one production step ending).
- Alex applies a customer's direction (steer acknowledged).
- Something requires the customer's attention (a check-in, a delay warning, a cancellation decision).
- A delay becomes unusual (past the expected duration for the operation type).

**Silence while useful work is visibly happening in Zone C is acceptable and preferred.** Chatbot-cadence narration is forbidden.

**Face state during Studio:**
- **Idle:** subtle life (blink, breath).
- **Actively working:** small ambient motion — soft breathing rhythm slightly more pronounced. Never a spinner or "thinking dots."
- **Waiting on customer:** face settles, expression slightly attentive.
- **After a steer:** brief acknowledgment expression (~500ms), then back to working.

**Copy rules for narration:**
- Present tense. First person. Warm.
- Never technical language (no *rendering, processing, job, queue, request, model, provider, brain, spinner*).
- Observations, not status reports.
- Honest about what happened. Do not fabricate quality assessments the system cannot make (see §7.6, §9.6).

### 3.2 Zone C — The piece

Zone C progresses through three honest presentation states across the customer's session. The customer always sees an accurate representation of what actually exists.

**State C-1 — The editable plan (drafting phase)**

Before any expensive media generation begins, Alex builds an editable plan. Zone C shows the plan structure — the customer sees exactly what will be made before any real production begins.

For a video, the plan includes:
- **Title** — top of Zone C. Editable inline. Starts as *Untitled*; Alex proposes a title silently once enough context exists.
- **Autosave status** — small status text next to the title: *Saved* / *Saving…* / *Couldn't save — retrying.* No timestamps.
- **Core message** — one line: what the video is about. Editable directly.
- **Script** — paragraphs of the actual dialogue. Directly editable (click to type). A nearby quiet affordance: **[Ask Alex to change this]** invokes AI revision for a selected span.
- **Visual direction** — a short text description of the intended visuals (e.g. *"Warm daytime footage of the storefront, close-ups of scoops, smiling customers, ending on the storefront sign."*). Editable. No fake preview frames.
- **Length** and **format** — surfaced as chips (see §3.3).
- **Call to action** — one line. Directly editable.

For a text-based piece, the plan is the piece itself: title, body paragraphs, CTA. Directly editable in place.

For a voiceover/audio piece, the plan includes: script (editable), voice choice (chip), tone (chip), length.

**State C-2 — Work underway (production phase)**

When the customer commits to production ([Make the video] — see §4), Zone C transitions:
- The **approved plan remains visible** — the customer can see what's being made and how it maps to the plan.
- A **quiet production status** replaces the top-of-plan action row: *"Making the finished video."*
- No progress bar. No percentage. Alex narrates meaningful transitions in Zone L only.
- The plan is **read-only during production** for elements being produced. Certain immediate steers (title, CTA) may remain editable (see §5).

**State C-3 — Finished media (preview state)**

When production completes, Zone C's plan display is replaced by the **finished piece**:
- Playable video with poster frame + explicit play affordance. **No autoplay. No hover-play.**
- Playable audio with waveform + play affordance.
- Finished text piece rendered in reading form.
- The customer can now enter Preview (see §6, State 12) for full-screen viewing.

**Rule:** Zone C never fabricates intermediate visual progress. If the backend cannot honestly show storyboards, partial renders, or interstitial frames, none are shown. What the customer sees always matches what actually exists.

**Inline editing rules:**
- **Text elements** (title, script, visual direction, CTA): clicking places a cursor for direct editing. Nearby **[Ask Alex to change this]** affordance available for AI-directed revision of any selection.
- **Visual segments** (once video is produced): tapping a segment opens a scoped steering prompt. Direct frame-level editing is not available.
- Direct edits are saved without ceremony; autosave status reflects.

### 3.3 Zone R — Contextual steering + primary action

Zone R has **two stacked regions**:

**Top region — Primary action (context-sensitive).**

The label and behavior of the primary action reflect the current phase (see §6 for the full progression):

- Drafting (plan forming): no dominant button. Zone R **[Steer Alex]** input is the primary interaction.
- Draft ready (plan complete): **[Review the plan]** — invites the customer to look at the finished draft.
- Production ready (after review): **[Make the video]** — commits to production.
- Production underway: **[Come back when it's ready]** — leaves Studio while work continues in the background. Secondary: **[Keep watching]** (stays) and, when possible, **[Stop making this]** (cancels — see §6, State 17).
- Media ready: **[Preview]** — enters preview mode.
- In preview (chrome inside Preview surface, not Zone R): **[Publish this]** and [Keep working].

**Middle region — Contextual controls (chips or compact pickers).**

Zone R shows **only the controls relevant to the current phase.** Studio is not a permanently visible configuration panel.

- **While drafting the plan:** chips for tone (2–4 options), length (short / medium / long), CTA (yes/no toggle if relevant).
- **While shaping the video plan** (after the script feels stable but before commitment): chips for format (vertical / square / horizontal), and if more than 5 options exist for a control (e.g., visual direction presets), a compact picker is used instead — do not force pickers into chip form when the set is too large.
- **While recording voice / producing audio:** chips for voice (with sample playback), delivery style.
- **After production completes (media ready):** revision-scoped controls appear if the customer wants to iterate: **[Change the opening]**, **[Change the CTA]**, **[Change the voice]** — each triggers a scoped steer with impact disclosure (see §5).

Chips are for choices with 2–5 clear alternatives. When a control legitimately has more, use a compact picker (a small dropdown or tap-to-open list). Do not force chip proliferation to preserve a rule.

**Bottom region — [Steer Alex] free-text input.**

- Persistent input at the bottom of Zone R.
- Label above input: *"Steer Alex"*
- Placeholder (contextual, changes with phase):
  - Drafting: *"Make the opening stronger…"*
  - Shaping video: *"Swap the first visual…"*
  - Media ready: *"Change the ending…"*
- Enter submits. No autocomplete suggestions in v1 (the placeholder demonstrates the interaction pattern).
- Input clears after submit; Alex's response appears in Zone L narration.

## 4. The commitment boundary

Studio has an explicit boundary between drafting (cheap and fast) and production (expensive and slower). The boundary is surfaced in the customer's language — not as *"cost"* but as a change in what changes cost.

### 4.1 Before the boundary — Drafting

- Alex builds the editable plan in Zone C.
- Every change is fast: chips update instantly, text edits save instantly, Alex-invoked revisions to the plan take seconds.
- No real media generation has happened yet.

### 4.2 The boundary — Approval

When the plan is complete, Alex signals in Zone L narration:

> *"I've got the message and structure in place. Ready for me to make the finished video?"*

At the same moment, Zone R's primary action becomes **[Review the plan]**. Tapping it enters a focused review — Zones L and R quiet down, Zone C is highlighted. When the customer is ready, Zone R primary becomes **[Make the video]** with a secondary [Keep drafting].

Committing to [Make the video] shows a short inline confirmation phrase — not a modal — beneath the primary action:

> *"I'll make the finished video now. This takes a bit longer than editing the plan."*

Two actions: **[Yes, make it]** (primary) and [Not yet] (secondary).

### 4.3 After the boundary — Production

- The plan is now approved.
- Alex begins real media generation.
- Zone C transitions to production state (§3.2 State C-2): plan visible, quiet production status.
- Zone R primary becomes **[Come back when it's ready]**.
- Immediate steers (title, CTA text) still work directly.
- Structural steers (voice, opening visual, duration) trigger impact disclosure (see §5).

The customer never sees the words *"cost,"* *"credits,"* *"tokens,"* *"tier,"* or *"minutes."* The behavioral change is disclosed in natural language.

## 5. Steer impact classification

Not every steer costs the same. Some are free-and-instant; some invalidate finished work; some invalidate the whole piece. The customer should understand impact before Alex acts on a heavy steer.

### 5.1 Classification (design-level; engineering-tuned specifics)

- **Immediate steers** — apply instantly, no confirmation:
  - Title change.
  - CTA text.
  - Tone wording (during drafting phase only).
  - Length nudge during drafting.
  - Small script edits (during drafting).

- **Partial-rebuild steers** — invalidate specific finished portions:
  - Voice change (during production or after).
  - Opening visual change (after production).
  - Duration adjustment (during production).
  - Script edits after production has begun.

- **Full-rebuild steers** — invalidate most or all finished work:
  - Format change (vertical ↔ horizontal ↔ square) after production.
  - Teammate change.
  - Language change after production.

### 5.2 Impact disclosure

For **immediate** steers: Alex applies silently or with a brief narration line if the change is meaningful.

For **partial-rebuild** steers: Alex explains impact and offers a choice.

> Alex: *"Changing the voice means I'll redo the voiceover. I can do that now, or keep this version."*
> Actions: **[Redo the voiceover]** (primary) / [Keep this version] (secondary)

For **full-rebuild** steers: Alex explains impact and offers a fork.

> Alex: *"That changes most of the video. I can remake it, or keep this version and start another one."*
> Actions: **[Remake it]** (primary) / [Keep this and start another] (secondary) / [Cancel] (quiet)

**[Keep this and start another]** preserves the current piece (saves it) and opens a new Studio session with the changed parameter. The customer's previous work is recoverable in Library.

### 5.3 Reversal

For any partial or full rebuild the customer confirms, a temporary **[Undo]** affordance appears in Zone L for ~30 seconds after the rebuild starts, in case they change their mind.

## 6. All states enumerated

### State 1 — Studio opens from Onboarding (Day 1)
- Face translates from Onboarding's top-centered position to Zone L over ~500ms.
- Zone C fades in with the plan skeleton (title area + placeholder sections).
- Zone R fades in with drafting-phase chips + [Steer Alex] input.
- Alex's first narration line lands: *"Alright — starting on your summer menu video. I'll get the plan together first."*
- Alex begins building the plan; sections fill in as Alex drafts them.

### State 2 — Studio opens from Home (returning to a draft)
- Full-screen dissolve from Home to Studio.
- Zone L, C, R fade in coordinated.
- Zone C shows the last saved state of the plan or media.
- Alex's greeting narration: *"Picking up your welcome video where we left off."*
- Alex offers to continue: *"Want me to keep going with what we had, or change direction?"*

### State 3 — Studio opens from Home (starting something new)
- Zone L shows Alex greeting: *"What are we making today?"*
- Zone C shows a **brief prompt-in-place** — a large input inviting a short description.
- Zone R shows a single [Steer Alex] input.
- Once the customer types a direction and submits, Zone C transitions to plan-skeleton and Alex begins drafting.

### State 4 — Alex is drafting the plan (collaborative — default)
- Plan sections appear in Zone C as Alex writes them.
- Narration in Zone L only at meaningful decision points.
- Zone R shows drafting-phase chips + [Steer Alex].
- No dominant primary action button; the interaction is the free-text steering input.

### State 5 — Customer steers during drafting
- Immediate steers (chip changes, direct text edits, small script tweaks) apply silently or with a brief narration line.
- Alex's contribution to the plan updates within seconds.

### State 6 — Alex asks a one-time collaboration question
- Once early in the first session, after Alex has made a first substantive decision (e.g., choosing the opening angle), Alex asks:
  > *"Want me to keep making the calls, or check with you as I go?"*
- Two inline replies: **[Keep going]** (stays autonomous-leaning) / **[Check with me]** (shifts to guided).
- The customer's answer sets Studio's cadence for the rest of the session.
- If ignored (no answer within a natural conversational pause), Alex proceeds with collaborative default; the question is not re-asked in the same session.
- This question is not re-asked in future sessions unless the customer's cross-session behavior signals a mismatch.

### State 7 — Alex has an unavoidable check-in (material decision)
- When a decision materially changes the result (e.g., choice between two very different visual directions), Alex asks and **waits** — no automatic continuation.
- Narration: *"Two directions to choose between here — a story-driven angle or a straightforward promo. Which feels right?"*
- Zone L shows inline **[Story-driven]** / [Straightforward] actions.
- Alex waits indefinitely. No 5-second timeout.
- If the customer wants to defer: **[Come back to this later]** — the piece pauses in draft state.

### State 8 — Draft ready (plan complete, awaiting review)
- Alex's narration: *"I've got the message and structure in place. Ready for me to make the finished video?"*
- Zone R primary action becomes **[Review the plan]**.
- Zone C's plan is complete and readable.

### State 9 — Reviewing the plan
- The customer taps [Review the plan].
- Zone L quiets; Zone R minimizes chips.
- Zone C is highlighted subtly; the plan is centered for focused reading.
- Zone R primary becomes **[Make the video]**. Secondary: **[Keep drafting]**.
- The customer can steer via [Steer Alex] or edit text directly at any time. Doing so returns to State 4.

### State 10 — Commitment (production confirmation)
- Customer taps [Make the video].
- Inline confirmation beneath the button: *"I'll make the finished video now. This takes a bit longer than editing the plan."*
- Actions: **[Yes, make it]** (primary) / [Not yet] (secondary).

### State 11 — Production underway
- Zone C transitions: plan remains visible; quiet production status appears at top: *"Making the finished video."*
- Zone R primary becomes **[Come back when it's ready]**. Secondary: **[Keep watching]** (stays visible).
- If provider supports cancellation: additional secondary **[Stop making this]** — see §11 State 17.
- If provider cannot cancel: additional secondary **[Leave and keep it running]** — same as [Come back when it's ready].
- Narration in Zone L is sparse — one line at start (*"Making the finished video now."*), one at completion.

### State 12 — Media ready
- Production completes; Zone C transitions to state C-3 (finished piece with play affordance).
- Zone L narration: *"It's ready. Want to preview?"*
- Zone R primary becomes **[Preview]**.
- Revision-scoped controls appear as secondary chips in Zone R (see §3.3 middle region).

### State 13 — Preview mode
- Full-screen takeover.
- The finished piece plays (video with poster + play; audio with waveform + play; text rendered).
- **Alex is entirely absent from the visible canvas.** No face, no name, no narration overlay.
- Chrome: **[Publish this]** (primary) and [Keep working] (secondary) at the bottom.
- Escape or dismiss returns to State 12; Alex returns.

### State 14 — Publish confirmation
- Sheet slides in from bottom (mobile) or as anchored side sheet (desktop) — never a centered modal.
- Content order (focus lands on the heading, not on any action):
  1. **Heading:** *"Ready to put this in front of your customers?"*
  2. **Destination:** *"Sending to: Download and shareable link."*
  3. **Primary action:** **[Yes, publish]**
  4. **Secondary action:** [Not yet]

### State 15 — Publishing
- Sheet closes; preview remains visible.
- Alex narrates once: *"Publishing now."*
- On success: transition to celebration on Home.
- Duration: target <3 seconds. If longer, Alex's narration adapts to the operation type (see §7.6).

### State 16 — Celebration transition
- Preview fades out.
- Home appears with the just-published piece in Zone C.
- Face persists across the wipe (from Preview's absence back to Home's Zone A position).
- Home's celebration sequence continues (per HOME_V1 State 1).

### State 17 — Cancellation during production
- Customer taps **[Stop making this]** during State 11 (only visible if provider supports cancellation).
- Alex confirms inline: *"I can stop here. The draft and plan will stay saved."*
- Actions: **[Stop]** (primary) / [Keep going] (secondary).
- On stop: production cancels, Zone C returns to State 8 (plan visible, [Review the plan] primary). The customer can re-commit later.

### State 18 — Leave during production (background continuation)
- Customer taps **[Come back when it's ready]** (or [Leave and keep it running] on providers that can't cancel).
- Alex says once: *"I'll finish this up. Home will let you know when it's ready."*
- Studio exits to Home.
- Home shows the piece in the in-progress state (per HOME_V1 State 3): the secondary action button reads **[Your summer video is still being finished]** with human narration copy.

### State 19 — Start over
- Customer types *"start over"* or invokes via the [Steer Alex] input.
- Inline confirmation appears in Zone L:
  > *"Start over with the same idea, or begin with a different one?"*
  > Actions: **[Same idea, fresh take]** / [Different idea] / [Cancel]
- **[Same idea, fresh take]:** current plan preserved as a recoverable version; Zone C clears; Alex starts a fresh draft with the same brief.
- **[Different idea]:** current plan preserved as recoverable; Zone C clears with a brief-prompt-in-place asking for the new direction.
- After restart, a temporary **[Undo start over]** affordance appears in Zone L for ~30 seconds.
- If not undone within 24 hours, the preserved version quietly moves to Library as a saved draft.

### State 20 — Show me options (scoped)
- Customer says *"show me options"* (or equivalent).
- Alex asks scope: *"Sure — what part do you want options for? The script? The opening line? The whole plan?"*
- Or, if the customer had a selection active (a paragraph, a segment, a title): Alex uses that as the scope automatically without asking.
- Once scope confirmed:
  - **Selected paragraph:** Alex offers 2–3 alternate paragraph versions.
  - **Selected video segment:** Alex offers 2–3 alternate direction or shot concepts.
  - **Title:** Alex offers 2–3 alternate titles.
  - **Whole plan:** Alex offers 2–3 alternate directions.
- Options display side-by-side (desktop) or as a swipeable stack (mobile).
- Each option has a **[Go with this]** action.
- Non-chosen options are preserved temporarily until the customer confirms a choice — the customer can compare/switch without regenerating.
- If the customer walks away (leaves Studio or opens another surface), non-chosen options are discarded.

### State 21 — Immediate steer during drafting (silent apply)
- Customer changes a chip (e.g., tone: playful → warm).
- Chip becomes active with subtle animation.
- Alex applies the change to the plan silently, or with a brief narration line if it meaningfully changes the draft: *"Warming up the tone."*
- Zone C reflects the change.

### State 22 — Partial-rebuild steer
- Customer changes a chip or types a steer that invalidates specific finished portions (e.g., voice change during production).
- Alex explains impact in Zone L narration:
  > *"Changing the voice means I'll redo the voiceover. I can do that now, or keep this version."*
- Actions: **[Redo the voiceover]** / [Keep this version].
- After confirmation: production adjusts; temporary [Undo] appears in Zone L for ~30s.

### State 23 — Full-rebuild steer
- Customer changes a parameter that invalidates most of the piece (format, teammate, language after production).
- Alex explains impact and offers fork:
  > *"That changes most of the video. I can remake it, or keep this version and start another one."*
- Actions: **[Remake it]** / [Keep this and start another] / [Cancel].
- **[Keep this and start another]:** current piece saved; new Studio session opens for the alternative.

### State 24 — Direct text edit (customer types in Zone C)
- Customer clicks a text element in Zone C (title, script paragraph, CTA).
- Cursor appears; direct editing enabled.
- Autosave status updates to *Saving…* → *Saved* after each pause in typing.
- Nearby quiet affordance: **[Ask Alex to change this]** — appears when a text selection exists. Tapping invokes AI-directed revision for the selected span.
- Alex is not a gate for text edits (IA-7).

### State 25 — Autosave state visible near title
- Location: right of the title in Zone C's top area.
- States: **Saved** (default), **Saving…** (during pending save), **Couldn't save — retrying.**
- No timestamps by default.
- On definitive save failure when the customer tries to leave Studio: see State 27.

### State 26 — Backend delay (operation-aware)
- Short drafting delay (~600ms+): narration line *"Thinking through the opening."*
- Media production (expected long): narration once at start (*"Making the finished video. You can stay here or come back when it's ready."*) — no continuous updates unless unusual.
- Unusual stall (past expected duration for the operation class): narration *"This is taking longer than it normally does. Want me to keep at it?"* + inline **[Keep going]** / [Come back later].
- No global timers. Delay messaging is keyed to expected-duration classes provided by the runtime for each operation type.

### State 27 — Definitive save failure on leave
- Customer taps [Back to Home] while a save is failing or has definitively failed.
- Confirmation sheet:
  > *"Your latest changes haven't been saved yet."*
  > Actions: **[Try again]** / [Leave without latest changes] / [Stay]
- Customer confirms behavior; Studio exits accordingly.

### State 28 — Generation partial failure (specific step failed)
- Alex narrates in honest language: *"I couldn't finish the voiceover. I'm trying it again."*
- Zone C shows the successful parts and a placeholder for the missing part.
- Automatic retry once; if retry fails, Alex offers alternatives:
  > *"I couldn't finish the voiceover after two tries. Want me to try a different voice, skip it for now, or come back to it?"*
- Actions inline in Zone L: **[Try a different voice]** / [Skip for now] / [Come back to it later].

### State 29 — Generation complete failure (backend unreachable)
- Alex narrates: *"I'm having trouble making this right now. Give me a moment, or come back to it later."*
- Zone C shows the plan (still intact) and a soft placeholder for the failed production.
- Zone R primary action: **[Try again]**. Secondary: [Come back later].
- The customer's brief, plan, and any partial work are preserved server-side.

### State 30 — Session lost / expired mid-session
- Silent redirect to `/sign-in` with `from` set so post-login returns to the same Studio state.
- All autosaved work preserved server-side.

## 7. Copy library

### 7.1 Opening narration

**From Onboarding transition:**
- *"Alright — starting on your summer menu video. I'll get the plan together first."*
- *"Alright — I'm working on your welcome video now. Let me lay out the plan."*

**Fresh piece from Home:**
- *"What are we making today?"*
- *"Ok — starting on your welcome message now."*

### 7.2 Drafting-phase narration (sparse)

Only when a meaningful decision is made or a new phase begins:

- *"I found a good angle — leading with your new gelato flavor."*
- *"Writing the opening line now."*
- *"Going with a warm, friendly tone."* (only if this is a meaningful decision; otherwise silent)
- *"Ok — that's the script drafted. Adding the visual direction."*

### 7.3 Commitment-boundary copy

- **Draft-ready invitation:** *"I've got the message and structure in place. Ready for me to make the finished video?"*
- **[Review the plan]** — primary in State 8.
- **[Make the video]** — primary in State 9.
- **Commitment confirmation:** *"I'll make the finished video now. This takes a bit longer than editing the plan."*
- **[Yes, make it]** / **[Not yet]**

### 7.4 Production narration (very sparse)

- Start of production: *"Making the finished video now."*
- Completion: *"It's ready. Want to preview?"*

### 7.5 Steer acknowledgment copy

**Immediate steers (chip changes, minor edits) — silent or brief:**
- (silent for most immediate changes)
- *"Warming up the tone."* (only when meaningful)
- *"Trimming it to about 15 seconds."*
- *"Ok — swapping the storefront shot for the counter."*

**Rule:** Alex does not echo the customer's steer back with *"You said X."* The submit itself is confirmation. Only echo when the steer is complex, when Alex needs to confirm interpretation, or when the steer triggers a partial or full rebuild.

**Complex steer echo (when confirmation is warranted):**
- *"Let me make sure I have this — you want a version aimed at repeat customers instead of new ones. That right?"* + inline **[Yes]** / [Not quite]

**Partial-rebuild copy:**
- *"Changing the voice means I'll redo the voiceover. I can do that now, or keep this version."* + actions

**Full-rebuild copy:**
- *"That changes most of the video. I can remake it, or keep this version and start another one."* + actions

### 7.6 Delay and failure copy

**Short drafting delay:**
- *"Thinking through the opening."*

**Expected long delay (media production):**
- *"Making the finished video. You can stay here or come back when it's ready."*

**Unusual stall:**
- *"This is taking longer than it normally does. Want me to keep at it?"* + **[Keep going]** / [Come back later]

**Partial failure (honest, not fabricated quality assessment):**
- *"I couldn't finish the voiceover. I'm trying it again."*
- *"I ran into trouble with the visuals. Let me try that once more."*
- **Never used:** *"The voiceover came out muddy"* or any quality diagnosis Alex cannot actually make.

**Persistent partial failure (after retry):**
- *"I couldn't finish the voiceover after two tries. Want me to try a different voice, skip it for now, or come back to it?"* + inline actions

**Complete failure:**
- *"I'm having trouble making this right now. Give me a moment, or come back to it later."* + **[Try again]** / [Come back later]

### 7.7 Interruption commands

- **"Start over"** → *"Start over with the same idea, or begin with a different one?"* + **[Same idea, fresh take]** / [Different idea] / [Cancel]
- **"Show me options"** → *"Sure — what part do you want options for? The script? The opening line? The whole plan?"*
- **"Stop making this"** (during production) → *"I can stop here. The draft and plan will stay saved."* + **[Stop]** / [Keep going]
- **"Come back later"** → *"I'll finish this up. Home will let you know when it's ready."*

### 7.8 Collaboration-question copy

- One-time question early in first session, after a first substantive decision:
  > *"Want me to keep making the calls, or check with you as I go?"*
- Inline **[Keep going]** / [Check with me].
- Not re-asked within the same session.
- Not re-asked in future sessions unless cross-session behavior signals mismatch.

### 7.9 Publish flow copy

- **Preview chrome:** **[Publish this]** / [Keep working]
- **Publish confirmation sheet:**
  - Heading: *"Ready to put this in front of your customers?"*
  - Destination: *"Sending to: Download and shareable link."*
  - **[Yes, publish]** / [Not yet]
- **Publishing state:** *"Publishing now."*
- **On success:** transition to Home celebration.

### 7.10 Back and autosave copy

- **Back affordance:** **[Back to Home]** — top-left.
- **Autosave status:** *Saved* / *Saving…* / *Couldn't save — retrying.* — next to the title in Zone C. No timestamps.
- **Never narrated during normal operation.** Alex does not say *"Saving your progress."* when autosave succeeds silently.
- **Save-failure-on-leave sheet:**
  > *"Your latest changes haven't been saved yet."*
  > Actions: **[Try again]** / [Leave without latest changes] / [Stay]

### 7.11 Placeholder copy for [Steer Alex] input

- Drafting: *"Make the opening stronger…"*
- Shaping video: *"Swap the first visual…"*
- Media ready: *"Change the ending…"*

Placeholders rotate through 2–3 contextual examples per phase; do not populate clickable suggestions in v1.

**Never used across all copy:** HTTP codes, `rendering`, `processing`, `job`, `queue`, `timeout`, `generation`, `request`, `model`, `provider`, `brain`, spinner text, percentages, credits, tokens, minutes, cost, tier, quality-assessment language Alex cannot actually make.

## 8. Motion & interaction notes

### 8.1 Studio opens from Onboarding

- Face translates from Onboarding's top-centered position to Studio's Zone L over ~500ms (smooth arc).
- Zone C fades in with the plan skeleton over ~600ms.
- Zone R fades in with drafting chips and [Steer Alex] input over ~500ms.
- First narration line lands as face reaches Zone L position.

### 8.2 Studio opens from Home

- Full-screen dissolve over ~400ms.
- No face translation.
- Zones L, C, R fade in coordinated.
- Alex's greeting narration lands as the layout settles.

### 8.3 Plan sections filling in Zone C

- Each plan section (title, core message, script paragraph, visual direction, CTA) fades in as Alex completes it (~250ms per fade).
- No typing animation.
- Autosave status updates unobtrusively.

### 8.4 Steer interactions

- **Chip change:** chip fill/color animates over ~200ms. Immediate steers: Zone C updates within 1–2s.
- **Typed steer (immediate):** input clears on submit; Alex applies silently or with brief narration.
- **Typed steer (partial or full rebuild):** input clears; narration + action buttons appear inline in Zone L over ~400ms.
- **Inline edit in Zone C:** cursor appears immediately on click; autosave status updates during typing.

### 8.5 Commitment boundary

- When plan is complete, Zone R's primary action button [Review the plan] fades in with a single soft pulse (one only, then still) over ~300ms.
- Tapping [Review the plan] transitions Zones L and R to a quieter state (~300ms fade), Zone C highlights subtly.
- Zone R primary updates to [Make the video]; secondary [Keep drafting] fades in.

### 8.6 Production start

- Customer taps [Yes, make it].
- Zone C's plan display transitions to production state over ~400ms — the plan remains visible but the top action row is replaced with the quiet production status.
- Zone R primary becomes [Come back when it's ready]; secondary [Keep watching] (and [Stop making this] or [Leave and keep it running]) fade in.

### 8.7 Preview transition (State 12 → State 13)

- **[Preview]** button pulses once when it appears.
- On tap: full-screen wipe expands Zone C to fill the viewport over ~500ms.
- Zones L and R fade out.
- **Alex is entirely removed from the surface** — face and narration both disappear during preview.
- **[Publish this]** and [Keep working] fade in at the bottom over ~300ms.

### 8.8 Publish confirmation (State 14)

- Sheet slides in over ~300ms.
- Preview dims to ~50%.
- **Focus moves to the sheet's heading** — not to the primary action.
- Order announced to screen readers: heading → destination → primary → secondary.

### 8.9 Publishing → celebration (States 15, 16)

- Sheet closes over ~200ms.
- Alex narrates once: *"Publishing now."* (narration appears briefly overlaid in the preview surface — a small text-only line, no face).
- On success: preview fades out over ~500ms; Home fades in beneath.
- Face reappears in Home's Zone A position.

### 8.10 Show me options (State 20)

- If Alex must ask scope, the question appears in Zone L; options render after the customer answers.
- Options: Zone C splits into 2–3 columns (desktop) or a swipeable stack (mobile) over ~400ms.
- Non-chosen options remain visible; **[Go with this]** on any activates it and fades the others out over ~400ms.
- Walking away discards all non-chosen options silently.

### 8.11 Reduced motion

- All translations reduce to instant swaps.
- Zone C section fills happen instantly rather than fading.
- Preview transition becomes an instant full-screen swap.
- Face-position translations become instant.

## 10. Accessibility notes

- **Focus on Studio open:** focus lands on Zone C's title area (or, for fresh-piece flow, on the brief input). Not on any Zone R action.
- **Keyboard:**
  - Tab moves through: Zone C interactive elements (title, editable text spans) → Zone R primary action → Zone R chips → Zone R [Steer Alex] input → [Back to Home].
  - Enter in [Steer Alex] submits.
  - Escape does not exit Studio (risks work loss). Escape may close the currently open sub-surface (preview, options split, publish sheet, inline confirmation).
  - Ctrl/Cmd-Z: supported for standard direct text undo within editable text spans (native browser behavior).
- **Screen reader:**
  - Alex's narration announces via `aria-live="polite"` — non-interrupting.
  - Zone C content described contextually: title, plan sections (announced by section name), then media form when produced.
  - Chips in Zone R announce their state (*"Short length, selected"*).
  - Steer acknowledgments announced via `aria-live="polite"`.
  - Autosave status updates announced via `aria-live="polite"` **only when changing to "Couldn't save — retrying"** — the standard *Saving…* / *Saved* transitions are visual-only.
  - Publish confirmation sheet: focus moves to the heading; order announced heading → destination → primary → secondary.
- **`prefers-reduced-motion`:** all transitions instant; sequences preserved.
- **Contrast:** narration text meets AAA (7:1). Chip text meets AA. Zone C plan text meets AA at minimum; long-form script content should meet AAA where possible.
- **Preview mode accessibility:** the finished piece must have appropriate accessible affordances (captions on video, transcript on audio, alt text on visual pieces). Deferred to media generation accessibility spec.

## 11. Persistence & autosave

Studio depends on durable autosave. The customer must be able to close a tab mid-work and resume without loss.

### 11.1 What is saved

- The brief.
- The plan (all sections).
- Direct text edits.
- Chip selections.
- Production state (queued, underway, complete, failed).
- Chosen and non-chosen variants during a "show me options" session (temporarily; discarded on session exit).
- Recoverable previous version after start-over (kept for 24 hours or until customer explicitly discards).

### 11.2 Autosave state visible near title

Status text right of the title:
- **Saved** (default).
- **Saving…** (during pending save; brief).
- **Couldn't save — retrying.** (transient failure being retried).

No timestamps. If the customer wants specifics, they can hover on desktop (a tooltip *"Last saved 2 minutes ago"*) — this is a nice-to-have, not v1 required.

### 11.3 Definitive save failure

If autosave fails and the retries exhaust:

- Status text becomes: **Couldn't save — retrying** (persists visibly).
- If the customer taps [Back to Home] in this state, a confirmation sheet appears (State 27) with three actions: **[Try again]** / [Leave without latest changes] / [Stay].
- If the customer stays, Studio continues to retry silently. When save eventually succeeds, status returns to *Saved.*

### 11.4 Recovery flows

- **Tab close / refresh:** on next session, Studio opens at the last saved state.
- **Session expired:** silent redirect to sign-in; post-login returns to Studio at the last saved state.
- **Background production during "come back when ready":** production continues server-side; Home reflects the in-progress state; on completion, Home notifies (via updated greeting and celebration).

## 12. Design constraints & trade-offs made explicit

**Trade-off 1 — Zone C shows honest states, not fabricated progress.**
Some products fake progressive video generation to keep customers engaged. We show the plan, then the finished media, and nothing pretending to be in-between. Cost: less "look at all this stuff happening." Benefit: trust preserved when the customer notices the plan doesn't animate into a video.

**Trade-off 2 — The commitment boundary is a real friction point.**
[Review the plan] → [Make the video] is two clicks between drafting and production. Faster products would eliminate this. We keep it because customers should understand when changes become expensive. Both clicks have clear language explaining what happens next.

**Trade-off 3 — Steers have visible impact classifications.**
Some products let every steer silently invalidate finished work. We surface impact for partial and full rebuilds. Cost: more copy, more decision moments. Benefit: customers understand consequence and control it.

**Trade-off 4 — Start over is recoverable, not destructive.**
Preserving the previous version means a small amount of storage per customer and a temporary [Undo] affordance. Justified because autosave sets an expectation of recoverability and destructive start-over would violate that trust.

**Trade-off 5 — Collaboration mode is not inferred from short-term inactivity.**
Some products would shift a quiet customer toward autonomy after 30s. We do not — inactivity is ambiguous (reading, distracted, waiting). Alex asks once early on; otherwise the default holds. Simpler, less presumptuous.

**Trade-off 6 — Narration is deliberately sparse.**
Chatbot-cadence narration would fill silence but reads as theater. Silence while useful work is visible is preferred. Alex speaks when there is something to say.

**Trade-off 7 — No echo of every steer.**
Some products confirm receipt by repeating input. We consider the submit itself the confirmation and reserve echo for cases where interpretation confirmation matters.

**Trade-off 8 — Zone R is context-sensitive, not always-full.**
Some products show every possible control at all times. We show only the controls relevant to the current phase. Cost: customers may look for a control that isn't visible. Benefit: Studio feels collaborative rather than like a configuration panel.

**Trade-off 9 — Preview removes Alex entirely.**
Some products keep the teammate present through preview for continuity. We remove Alex so the customer can judge the work exactly as the audience will. Continuity is preserved by Alex reappearing on Home immediately after publish.

**Trade-off 10 — Delay messaging is operation-aware, not global.**
Requires engineering to expose expected-duration classes per operation type. Justified because a global timer produces false alarms for expected-long operations (video production) and slow reassurance for expected-short ones (small text edits).

**Trade-off 11 — Cancellation may not be available for all operations.**
The [Stop making this] affordance is conditional on provider capability. When cancellation isn't possible, [Leave and keep it running] is the honest alternative. Never promise stopping when the provider can't stop.

## Revision History

**v1 — 2026-08-06.** First canonical version. Written and revised during the same session against operator direction. Major decisions incorporated:

- **Honest presentation states:** Zone C shows editable plan → work underway (plan remains visible) → finished media. Never fabricates intermediate media progress.
- **Plan-first drafting:** editable plan (message, script, visual direction, length, format, CTA) forms in Zone C before any real media generation begins.
- **Commitment boundary:** explicit *"I've got the plan in place. Ready for me to make the finished video?"* moment. Primary actions: [Review the plan] → [Make the video]. Behavioral boundary disclosed in natural language, not cost/credit vocabulary.
- **Steer classification:** immediate (silent), partial-rebuild (impact disclosed + choice), full-rebuild (impact disclosed + fork). Rebuild options preserve current version.
- **Start over recoverable:** [Same idea, fresh take] / [Different idea] / [Cancel]. Prior version preserved; [Undo start over] affordance for ~30s; auto-moves to Library after 24h if not undone.
- **Collaboration spectrum revised:** begins collaborative. No aggressive behavioral inference. Alex asks once early on: *"Want me to keep making the calls, or check with you as I go?"*
- **Narration frequency:** sparse. Only meaningful decisions, phase changes, applied direction, attention needs, unusual delays. Silence-during-useful-work preferred.
- **No steer echoing:** customer steers not repeated back unless complex or triggering rebuild.
- **Zone R context-sensitive:** shows only phase-relevant controls. Chips for 2–5 options; compact pickers when larger. Never force chip proliferation.
- **[Steer Alex] label:** replaces *"Tell me anything."* Contextual placeholders per phase.
- **Inline editing (text):** direct click-to-edit. Nearby **[Ask Alex to change this]** for AI-directed revision.
- **Preview removes Alex:** face and narration entirely absent. Customer judges the work as the audience will.
- **Publish focus order:** heading → destination → primary → secondary. Focus lands on heading, not on any action.
- **[Back to Home]:** language preserved with persistent *Saved* status nearby. No repeated narration for successful autosaves.
- **Visible autosave state:** *Saved* / *Saving…* / *Couldn't save — retrying.* — near the title. No timestamps. Save-failure-on-leave sheet with three actions.
- **Draft-vs-production readiness distinguished:** [Review the plan] → [Make the video] → [Preview] → [Publish this]. Each action names what happens next.
- **Background production supported:** [Come back when it's ready] leaves Studio; Home reflects in-progress state.
- **Operation-aware delay messaging:** no global timers. Runtime provides expected-duration classes per operation type.
- **Honest failure language:** no fabricated quality assessments. Alex says what actually happened.
- **Show me options scoped:** operates on the customer's selection (paragraph, segment, title, whole plan). Alex asks scope only if no selection. Non-chosen options preserved during the session.
- **Cancellation offered when supported:** [Stop making this] when provider supports it; [Leave and keep it running] otherwise.
- **Primary action always exists:** during active work, [Steer Alex] IS the primary interaction. Never depends on passive waiting.
- **Trade-offs made explicit** across honest state presentation, commitment boundary, impact classification, recoverable start-over, invisible collaboration, sparse narration, context-sensitive Zone R, preview absence of Alex, operation-aware delays, and conditional cancellation.
